    Data collection

    • SYNAPATH AI is the closest direct analogue to what we are building, and it just raised at angel stage. Nearly RMB 100M, roughly $14.9M, for a product matrix spanning Ego, Exo and Tactile. Claimed accumulation is hundreds of thousands of hours, over 36 million entries, across more than 260 scenario categories. The part that matters is the customer list: X Square Robot, Fourier Intelligence and ROBOTERA, plus unnamed large internet companies. That is a capture-only company selling straight into the top tier of Chinese VLA labs, and it did not surface in English-language search at all. Gasgoo
    • GenRobot.AI closed a Series A led by Momenta, its seventh consecutive round in a single year, taking total raised past $200M on body-agnostic human data. The hardware is Gen DAS, a non-intrusive wearable collection device, plus a Gen Controller, with sensing across sound, magnetic tactile, piezoresistive, capacitive, flexible fabric and force feedback. Earlier in 2026 it open-sourced a 13,000+ hour body-agnostic dataset now doing over 1 million downloads a month, and its Data Foundation Model covers chain-of-thought, 6D pose, depth, tactile, hand tracking and full-body mesh. Momenta leading is the tell. An autonomy company is buying into the capture layer rather than building it. The open-sourcing move is the one to think hardest about, because giving away 13,000 hours to own the format is a strategy we do not currently have an answer to. Gasgoo, 36Kr
    • Transfyr launched with $25M led by General Catalyst to capture human lab work as robot training data. Lux Capital, Breakout Ventures, Factory, Neo, SV Angel, MVP Ventures, Underscore VC and Lyda Hill all in. Founders are Anna Marie Wagner, previously head of AI and corporate development at Ginkgo Bioworks, and Renee Wegrzyn, founding director of ARPA-H. The platform passively captures lab work with integrated sensors and multimodal models, recording operator actions and intent, environmental conditions and equipment data, and structures it to train on how experiments actually get run rather than on the written protocol. Same shape as our pitch, pointed at one high-value vertical instead of the general world, and funded by a tier-one generalist. The vertical-first framing is worth stealing. The AI Insider
    • Ropedia shipped HOMIE Gen2, its fourth generation of capture hardware in twelve months, on $30M total funding. Head-worn, 380g, 360-degree visual coverage, 1080p at 30fps global shutter, four-channel spatial audio, and a multifunction sync port for multi-unit rigs and extra sensors. Recordings run through a Human Experience Engine that synchronizes 10+ data types including video, audio, depth, movement, body pose and spatial, and the company claims up to 96% accuracy interpreting real-world human activity. CEO Zhaoxi Chen’s public argument is that fixed cameras and controlled demonstrations cannot produce in-the-wild diversity. Four hardware generations in a year is a cadence we should be measuring ourselves against, and the 96% number needs a definition before anyone quotes it back at us. The AI Insider
    • Perceptron’s Isaac 0.5 puts ego video in a frontier model’s disclosed training mix. Founded November 2024 by Armen Aghajanyan and Akshat Shrivastava, both ex-Meta FAIR, and the model is a general-purpose vision model for perceive, reason and act in warehouses and factories, released open-weight and currently top of Hugging Face’s trending robotics list. Shrivastava describes petabyte-scale datasets running from images and text and video through to robot trajectories. TechCrunch carries a correction note on the funding details in this piece, so do not print a round size for Perceptron. The training-mix disclosure is the citable part. TechCrunch, Hugging Face
    • Riemann-1.0 is a world action model pretrained on more than 200,000 hours of interaction data that explicitly unifies egocentric human video, handheld-gripper demonstrations and heterogeneous robot trajectories under one objective. Reported results are 94.3% on RoboTwin2.0, 99.0% on LIBERO and 62.6% on RoboCasa-365 for a gain of 8.4% over the previous best, plus 85.0% success and 94.4% progress success on long-horizon real-world manipulation, beating the strongest open-source baseline by 15 points. If one training objective genuinely absorbs ego human video and robot trajectories without a retargeting stage, the format question for a capture business gets simpler and the volume question gets bigger. arXiv
    • Zero-WAM uses a human video as the task prompt and releases HumanGen, 74.2K human-robot in-context-learning pairs across 8.6K tasks. On seven unseen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks it hits 47.0% average success, 29.5 points over the strongest video-action baseline. The pairs were generated automatically by converting robot trajectories into semantically matched human videos, which is the reverse of what we sell, and worth watching as a substitute. arXiv
    • A fiber-optic sensing glove hits 7.2mm mean fingertip position error against motion capture, dropping to 4.9mm after a one-time factory calibration of the routing hub that transfers across users and sessions. Multi-core shape-sensing fibers capture each fiber’s full 3D shape rather than curvature alone, with a new IK solver reconstructing full hand pose at 60Hz, benchmarked on a two-hour dataset across five subjects. The transferable calibration is the commercial detail. It means the glove does not need per-user setup, which is the thing that kills throughput on any capture programme run by non-experts. arXiv
    • MiGA is a multi-gripper dataset spanning five distinct gripper types across multiple robots with 103,000 demonstrations, built because essentially all existing VLA data is parallel-jaw. The companion GVLA model adds a multi-gripper tokenizer and adapter-based policy routing. There are no success-rate numbers in the abstract, so treat it as a dataset contribution. The useful read is that end-effector diversity is now a named gap in the training corpus, and a capture programme that varies the tool is collecting something scarce. arXiv
    • UCAG-P trains one camera-centric action schema across robot arms, humanoids and human hands with no retargeting, on 4.03K hours of robot and simulation data plus 2.34K hours of human demonstrations. A single checkpoint with no benchmark-specific fine-tuning gets 98.3% LIBERO, 88.7% and 89.2% on RoboTwin Easy and Hard, 82.0% zero-shot on LIBERO-Plus and 62.0% on RoboCasa GR-1. Note the ratio: human demonstration hours are more than a third of the robot and sim hours in a frontier-grade pretraining mix. arXiv
    • LM-X discloses a training budget worth writing down: more than 20,000 hours of real-robot trajectories including over 1,000 hours of failed policy rollouts, in a 20-day run on 64 NVIDIA B200 GPUs. Final results are 74.1% across 50 randomized-hard RoboTwin2.0 tasks against 55.4% for GR00T N1.7, and 68.6% against 50.7% on seven real-robot tasks. The 1,000 hours of deliberately retained failures is the second paper in two weeks to price failure data as an asset. arXiv
    • A survey on smart glasses as first-person intelligence platforms lays out an L0 to L5 capability framework, eight hardware capability axes, a nine-dimensional deployment framework and a claim-conditioned evaluation protocol. No numbers, it is a survey. It is useful as a citation that frames wearable first-person capture as a systems problem rather than a model problem, which is the argument we make to buyers who think a GoPro is the same product. arXiv
    • A staffer from Morphi Robot said at WRC that wider egocentric data collection has made household data cheaper and more abundant, and named an emerging rule of thumb of pretraining on tens of thousands of hours before task fine-tuning. Household demonstrations at the show still took 10 to 15 minutes per task. Separately Coowa disclosed over 10,000 robots in routine operation across 50+ cities generating more than 50 petabytes of real-world data. Two things to hold together: the price of ego hours is falling because supply is arriving, and the pretraining floor buyers quote is now tens of thousands of hours. Both compress margin on undifferentiated capture. KrASIA

    Data annotation & validation

    • Leju earmarked roughly RMB 616M of a RMB 2.6B ChiNext IPO raise for high-quality large-scale datasets. That is a public company putting a line item on a prospectus for buying training data, at a RMB 4.327B post-investment valuation, and it is the hardest single number available for the data-market TAM case. Leju has also made 13 external investments, four in core components, six in software and intelligence, three in scenario joint ventures, and the investee list includes Embodied Data and Yunkehang, both training-data companies. Underlying business: 577 Kuavo units in 2025 for about RMB 178M revenue, roughly 12x year on year and about 70% of total, with gross margin falling from 50.45% to 40.78% and cumulative three-year net loss over RMB 170M. Robot margins compressing while the data budget gets its own IPO line is the whole thesis in one filing. 36Kr
    • WorldEcho breaks the assumption that a world model can stand in for a real evaluator. It probes action-following over off-expert action distributions using visual integrity and SE(3) trajectory alignment, and finds current world models execute expert actions reasonably but ignore commanded actions or produce visually invalid rollouts once you leave the expert distribution. No numbers in the abstract. Anyone selling simulated evaluation is selling something that works exactly where you did not need it. arXiv
    • InstructMove argues most manipulation benchmarks are contaminated, because the target object is usually visually salient or uniquely feasible, so a policy can score without reading the instruction. It enforces that multiple actions are visually and physically plausible with only one matching the language, and decomposes instruction-following into category identification, attribute discrimination, spatial reasoning and compositional pick-and-place. No numbers in the abstract. This is a benchmark-design paper that doubles as an annotation spec, because those four axes are exactly the label taxonomy a language-grounded dataset needs. arXiv
    • A resilience-metrics paper evaluates 400 household tasks across 10 embodied agent systems and shows success rate hides everything that matters. It proposes Rebound, Stability and Graceful Extensibility, and finds recovery-cost differences among episodes that all counted as successes, reporting a delta of 25.2. Process-level metrics need process-level labels, which is a higher-margin product than outcome labels. arXiv
    • ConfAL-WM is a labeling-budget-allocation method: a lightweight confidence probe on UNet decoder features predicts dense latent confidence maps, aggregated to task, frame and patch level to drive data selection and localized training. Evaluated on RoboTwin2.0 against scalar reward, progress and judge-based scoring baselines, no numbers in the abstract. Patch-level annotation targeting is the direction the tooling is going, and it is a rule for deciding which frames of ego footage are worth paying a human to look at. arXiv
    • TrapVLA demonstrates a backdoor where the attacker controls how the robot fails, not merely that it fails, for instance grasping with a specified positional offset. It ships a data engine for synthesizing target trajectories, an automated fidelity measurement suite for configured failures, and two benchmarks, Trap-LIBERO and Trap-RoboTwin, across four failure modes. No success-rate numbers in the abstract. Read it as a data-supply-chain risk. If poisoned demonstrations can specify a failure mode, provenance attestation stops being a nice-to-have clause and becomes a product. arXiv
    • Two egocentric evaluation benchmarks landed on the same day and both point at annotation rather than pixels. EgoErrorVQA is the first benchmark for egocentric procedural-error comprehension, with an evaluator agent built on the Agent2Agent protocol, and finds persistent weaknesses in handling procedural errors. EgoArgus is a human-annotated egocentric dataset across five dialogue-video daily scenarios testing whether a model can arbitrate when visual evidence and user language conflict, and finds existing modality-bias mitigation quite restricted. Neither carries numbers in the abstract. Procedural error and modality conflict are both label classes, and neither is in any capture contract I have seen. arXiv, arXiv
    • A pre-registered study of budget-constrained embodied perception across seven open-weight models from 3B to 31B reports that its own architecture fails to validate. Under a 4,096-token decision budget, query-conditioned access reaches 75% to 94% episodic retrieval accuracy against 3% to 19% for equal-budget query-independent sampling, and budget reallocation beats quadrupling the sampling budget on every backbone. Then the authors report that removing their compressive state raises the flagship mean from 35.4 to 58.0, and that two of four pre-registered falsification criteria fire. Pre-registration in robotics evaluation is new and this is the first paper I have read that let its own null result through. Worth citing for that alone. arXiv
    • Carbon Robotics introduced a plant foundation model plus a tractor kit for in-field customization of laser weeding, with iMerit as the annotation partner. Separate item from the EXL close covered on Monday. iMerit showing up as the data partner inside a customer’s foundation-model product, rather than as a labeling vendor behind it, is the shape mid-tier annotation companies are trying to move into post-acquisition. The Robot Report
    • Surge AI scored DeepSeek V4 Pro at 59.7 on the Tuesday Work Index, ahead of Qwen 3.8 Max at 58.7, its third index post in ten days. The score is not the signal. The cadence is. Surge is running a recurring benchmark franchise, which is a positioning move from labeling vendor to evaluation authority, and it is the move every annotation company will now try. Surge AI
    • At Actuate, a booth for Avala advertised solving “the robotics data crisis” and Foxglove announced natural-language semantic search over robot data built on NVIDIA’s Cosmos open-weight world model. The conference drew 1,500 attendees, tripled since 2023. Foxglove CEO Adrian Macneil’s line is the useful one, that there will not be a ChatGPT moment for robotics and he would be excited for an Apple II moment. Search-over-robot-data is the tooling layer forming above raw storage, and it needs semantic labels to work. TechCrunch

    • PredVLA is the strongest “you may not need scale” datapoint of the window: 0.68 million trainable parameters, no robot-data pretraining, 86.9% mean success across the three short-horizon LIBERO suites and 75.4% including the long-horizon suite. Under a controlled comparison with identical frozen front end, demonstrations, action decoder and protocol, it reaches 3.7x and 7.4x the mean success of parameter-matched Transformer and LSTM policies. A sub-million-parameter policy is embedded-deployable on hardware that costs nothing. Hold this one honestly, because it is an argument against our own volume pitch and buyers will find it. arXiv
    • FlashVLA holds multiple action chunks at different noise levels in a streaming buffer and decodes with chunk-wise causal attention, emitting one executable chunk per inference step at 30Hz or better on a single GPU. No extra future-state conditioning required. 30Hz on one GPU is the threshold where a VLA becomes a controller rather than a demo. arXiv
    • LAWA keeps test-time future imagination but drops the future-video branch at inference, getting 65.6% few-shot and 80.8% full-data on RoboCasa, up 9.6 and 4.5 points over the matched Fast-WAM baseline, at 42.9% lower inference latency. Matching joint world-action model performance without paying the inference cost is the pattern the whole field is converging on this week. arXiv
    • Pointing-VLA replaces autoregressive text-coordinate spatial grounding with typed hidden-state readout heads and reduces recorded controller time by more than 20x, with the typed heads running 6.68x to 6.90x faster than Embodied-R1 text decoding. It gets 72.9% average on the four-task Bridge/WidowX set with no Bridge-specific finetuning, and used as spatial guidance for a pi-0.5 policy it moves real-robot autonomous success from 52.7% to 80.7% across three visual contexts. arXiv
    • CRESSim-Neo reaches up to 2.03 million environment steps per second for 8,192 parallel CartPole environments on a single RTX 4090, with a GPU-resident pipeline covering rigid bodies, deformable tissue, fluids and strands and zero-copy PyTorch integration via DLPack. Alongside it, NVIDIA’s Cosmos-H-Dreams distills a bidirectional action-conditioned surgical video world model, fine-tuned on the Open-H-Embodiment corpus, into a causal few-step student streaming at about 160 inference FPS on a single RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. Cosmos-H-Dreams is controller-agnostic, demonstrated with a browser keyboard over WebRTC, Meta Quest over WebXR and CMR Surgical’s Versius console, and released open. Surgical is the vertical where world models are getting real first, and it is one we have a credible route into through Indian hospitals. arXiv, arXiv
    • ROS2SmolVLA adapts Hugging Face’s SmolVLA to Universal Robots hardware and releases an open-source ROS 2 interface, validated on a UR10e pick-and-place. No numbers in the abstract. The motivation is the interesting part, stated as on-premise compute for compliance and security reasons, which is the same argument that makes Indian pharma buyers want the model to stay inside the plant. arXiv
    • Quantized VLA and VLN checkpoints are now routine on Hugging Face. InternVLA-N1-DualVLN shipped NVFP4 and FP8 quantizations on 25 August. Read alongside Xiaomi shipping three robotics checkpoints in about two weeks, GigaAI’s GigaBrain-0.7-3.5B-Base and Perceptron’s Isaac 0.5, open-weight VLA releases are now near-daily and increasingly Chinese-origin. Hugging Face

    • SOLO deploys omni-terrain humanoid locomotion zero-shot with only a chest-mounted depth camera and proprioception, completing a continuous 1.5km outdoor route. That stepping-stone gap, 96% against near zero, is the kind of number that decides whether a humanoid leaves the flat factory floor. arXiv
    • TOWN-VLA buries the most alarming result of the week in its motivation section: naively appending retrieved text to a frozen VLA’s prompt drops mean success from 92.47% to 3.00%, and both meaningful and length-matched meaningless appends fail on all 500 states. Changing instruction form dominates semantics. Their prompt-authority interface then moves success from 69.5% to 73.1% on a matched LIBERO-Plus evaluation over 10,030 episodes per method, and on a physical PiPER arm with a frozen pi-0.5 checkpoint from 52.7% to 78.7% over 150 trials per method at p = 3.16e-6. If prompt formatting alone can zero a policy, then instruction phrasing is a labeled field in the dataset and not free text. arXiv
    • Four papers improved VLA success by feeding geometry or intent back into the action expert, and the out-of-distribution deltas are where to look. V-Link over GR00T N1.6 gets +1.9% on LIBERO but +31.2% on LIBERO-Plus and +18.8% on RoboTwin 2.0, plus +20% and +24% on two real tasks on an AgiBot A3 Ultra humanoid. GaussianDream++ keeps only 20 world tokens at inference and hits 98.6% LIBERO, 87.8% LIBERO-Plus, and moves real-robot average from 29.2% to 52.5% over a reproduced pi-0.5. INDI distills intent from a frozen teacher VLM and takes GR00T-N1.7 from 64.3% to 84.7% on SimplerEnv-Bridge and 62.0% to 68.7% real-world. GaussVLA gets 93.5% LIBERO average and 100.0% on Spatial with 200M parameters, a 19.7% relative gain over SpatialVLA. The in-distribution numbers are saturated. The generalization suites are where the remaining signal is, and they are the ones sensitive to data diversity. arXiv, arXiv, arXiv, arXiv
    • TrAct uses embodiment-agnostic visual tracks as the interface between control and world-model prediction, improving success from 27% to 55% in simulation on LIBERO-INTEGRAL and 49% to 76% on real-world Franka tasks against a pi-0.5 baseline. UniMem adds single-backbone multimodal memory for non-Markovian tasks, getting 93.4% against 68.2% for fixed-interval image sampling in simulation and 80.0% against 43.5% for hierarchical VLM baselines in hardware, with faster inference. arXiv, arXiv
    • CLAP trains action-conditioned video world models across human and robot video by reconciling action spaces through end-effector poses, language and latent actions, with a curriculum that learns physical priors from unlabeled video before grounding. Claims to approach or surpass state of the art single-embodiment video models on DROID and releases the most comprehensive suite to date across cross-embodiment, DROID, Bridge, bimanual YAM and G1 humanoids, fully open-sourced. No specific numbers in the abstract. The mechanism, learning physics from unlabeled human video then grounding into end-effector space, is the cheapest possible use of raw ego footage. arXiv
