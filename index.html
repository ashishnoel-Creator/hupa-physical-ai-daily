    Data collection

    November 2 — Chef Robotics’ training pipeline. The company says its deployed robots have accumulated 150 million pick experiments. Each useful training example connects measured pre-pick depth, the action, actual scale weight and post-pick depth. Its account describes content-addressed computation, exact data/code provenance and retryable processing; a rebuild uses more than ten times the previous training corpus. These are company-reported scale and infrastructure claims, not independent accuracy results. Interpretation: outcome measurements and reproducible processing can be part of the collection product, alongside images. Technical article.

    Newly discovered, original publication date not disclosed — RewardAI OM-1. The company’s human-worn capture system combines seven-degree-of-freedom hand information, in-hand global-shutter images, tactile/proximity sensing, force and electromagnetic/visual-inertial tracking. It claims human-only training and cross-embodiment policy transfer. Treat that as a company demonstration, not independently established generalization; no commissioned collection opening was verified. Original technical account.

    October 29–30 research catch-up: CIMI transfers human contact geometry; HandMimicX uses ego RGB-D object motion. WorldBridge finds video-only examples and sparse action-labelled demonstrations complementary. Capture modality and supervision must match the intended training method.

    Data annotation & validation

    October 29 — offline scores are not enough. A long-horizon dexterity study evaluates 281 policies across roughly 1,700 physical RJ45-connector trials. Only five of 67 tested offline proxies correlate within one policy family. The result is task-specific, but it supports retaining physical evaluation and separating representation changes from apparent dataset-size gains. Study.

    October 30 — explicit negative and recovery tests. MolmoSpaces CommonSense stresses clutter, occlusion and object choice, including failures of model-based judges. VLAs Do Not Know When Not to Act finds that six evaluated checkpoints still act under empty instructions in a simulated benchmark. Neither establishes a field-wide failure rate. Both motivate annotating wrong-object actions, no-action conditions and recovery outcomes rather than scoring plausible-looking motion alone.
