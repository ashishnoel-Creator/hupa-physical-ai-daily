# August 2026 review

August separated the capture layer from the robot layer and priced them in opposite directions. Unitree listed, ran up 460%, then gave back half its market cap inside three sessions while its founder talked his own timeline down. In the same four weeks four capture-only companies got funded, XPeng put $900M into a humanoid unit and said out loud that the point is data rather than robots, and Dyna published a human-to-robot scaling law over a million hours of egocentric video. Bodies got cheaper and got repriced down. Hours got scarcer at the top of the quality curve and got repriced up.

*Coverage note: this synthesizes 15 day briefs from August 1 to August 28. Three of them (August 10, 24 and 28) were catch-up runs covering multi-day windows, and no brief ran August 29 to 31, so anything that landed in the last three days of the month is not in here.*

## TL;DR

- **Dyna Robotics pre-trained Dyna-2 on more than 1M hours of egocentric human video and published a human-to-robot scaling law running across four orders of magnitude, with the curve holding on zero-shot evaluation against robot data the model never saw.** Human ego hours now have a published elasticity attached to them. Hold the specific numbers loosely, the release carries a publisher correction. [August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)
- **The capture layer got funded four separate times in five days at month end, and none of the four is a robot company.** SYNAPATH AI took nearly RMB 100M selling ego, exo and tactile data straight into X Square Robot, Fourier and ROBOTERA. GenRobot.AI closed a Series A led by Momenta, its seventh round in a year, past $200M total. Transfyr launched with $25M from General Catalyst to instrument human lab work. Ropedia shipped its fourth head-worn rig in twelve months. [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)
- **SoftVTBench held FEM state back as evaluator-only ground truth and found that every single in-distribution configuration tested contained "successful" rollouts that had damaged the object, between 0.7% and 24% of each configuration's successes.** Binary task success is not a safe label. That is the sharpest technical argument for validated delivery anyone published this year, and it came from a benchmark paper with no commercial stake. [August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html)
- **What should change in our plan: stop selling hours and start selling the label.** The wedge got crowded in one month, and the arguments that survive the crowd are all about the delivery rather than the volume. EU AI Act Article 10 went live August 2 and made documented collection origin and annotation process a legal requirement. TrapVLA made provenance a security problem instead of a compliance one. GS-VLA showed a small camera-mount shift drops policy success from about 90% to about 10%. SiMDex mined under 5% of a 32M-sample pool and beat equal-size random sampling by 13 points. [August 2](../days/2026-08-02.html), [August 10](../days/2026-08-10.html), [August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)
- **Watch in September: Addverb.** Sangeet Kumar told Business Standard the company trains on 1,000 hours a week today and plans to reach 5,000. That is a Reliance-controlled domestic buyer with a published appetite of 4,000 incremental hours a week, and it is the first hard demand-side number ever to come out of India. Nobody announced they were serving it. [August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html)

## Biggest moves by theme

### Data collection

The month's arc is that capture stopped being a feature of robot companies and became a fundable business with its own customer lists. On August 1 the funded capture assets were rig makers and a handful of operators. By August 28 SYNAPATH AI was selling ego, exo and tactile data into three named Chinese VLA labs at angel stage, GenRobot.AI had raised past $200M across seven rounds in a single year on a wearable device and an open corpus, Transfyr had $25M from General Catalyst for a vertical capture play in scientific labs, and INFIFORCE had closed nearly RMB 1B on a stated ego-native strategy with state platforms on the ticket ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html), [August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html)). Momenta leading GenRobot is the tell worth sitting with. An autonomy company with its own fleet decided to buy into the capture layer rather than build it.

Capture-at-work went from a startup thesis to platform infrastructure inside four weeks, and it happened almost entirely in China. Tau Robotics rents teleoperated cleaning humanoids in San Francisco at $30 an hour and keeps every full-session video indefinitely, which is capture at negative acquisition cost ([August 3](../days/2026-08-03.html)). Tacta shipped a glove with 256 tactile sensors per fingertip that factory workers wear during normal work ([August 10](../days/2026-08-10.html)). Moqi's founder, ex-Huawei, described hiring cleaners to wear lightweight rigs in hotels and apartments and putting strict screening in front of the output, on a billion yuan of angel money ([August 11](../days/2026-08-11.html)). Then the platforms arrived. OneRobotics signed 58.com to turn its lifestyle-services workforce into a collection network, and two days later OriginFlow named 58 Group as the partner for gathering housekeeping data through EMG sensing ([August 17](../days/2026-08-17.html), [August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html)). Two different capture stacks wired into the same workforce inside five days. JD did the same with DexTeleop across Seven Fresh supermarket floors ([August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html)).

Underneath that the rig hit a floor price and the premium moved somewhere else. Ego-OSCAR published a sub-$200 bill of materials for an open-source head-mounted stereo-inertial rig and shipped 550 crowdsourced hours with it, calling itself the cheapest defensible substrate for crowdsourced ego capture ([August 11](../days/2026-08-11.html)). ViHaTeleop did a $550 visual-haptic rig with measured yield gains on every task ([August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html)). If a defensible rig costs $200, hardware is not the moat. What is left is sync quality, channel coverage, distribution of environments and the audit trail, which is exactly what Ropedia is building toward with HOMIE Gen2, four hardware generations in twelve months, 380g, 10+ synchronized data types ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Physen AI is further along commercially than anyone expected, with a supply partnership with Figure and integrations running with Amazon and DoorDash ([August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html)). And Orbbec turns out to be quietly building the arms supplier to the whole industry, selling body-agnostic capture platforms white-label through full contract manufacturing to 5,000+ enterprise clients including 1,600 robotics companies ([August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html)).

The counter-pressure got much stronger this month and we should say so plainly. Tencent released Hy-Embodied-0.5 with the corpus attached, 10,000+ hours of UMI human demonstrations across roughly 1M episodes, Apache-2.0, with the capture-to-RL pipeline included ([August 12](../days/2026-08-12.html)). GenRobot open-sourced 13,000+ hours doing over a million downloads a month, which is giving away the hours to own the format. NVIDIA refreshed Open-H-Embodiment at 750 hours from 33 contributing organizations ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). A Morphi staffer said at WRC that wider ego collection has made household data cheaper and more abundant, and that the emerging rule of thumb is pretraining on tens of thousands of hours before task fine-tuning ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Raw hours are deflating. The good news arrived in the same month from SiMDex, which mined 1.49M task-relevant samples out of a 32M ego pool, under 5%, and lifted real success from 47.7% to 61.1% against equal-size random sampling ([August 10](../days/2026-08-10.html)). The value is not the hours. It is knowing which hours.

The conversion pipelines also left the lab. Ego2Robot published 18,561 hours across 15 morphologies, RoboEdit shipped 174K aligned pairs and 14M frames from plain RGB with no mocap and no teleop rig, HandEdit put out 200M instances of human-to-robot hand editing, and Hydra-0's inverse mode produced executable robot motion from human demonstration flow with no task-specific robot demos ([August 5](../days/2026-08-05.html), [August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html), [August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html), [August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html)). Every one of these bounds its output quality at the source footage. That is the argument for shooting to spec, and it now has four separate papers behind it.

### Data annotation & validation

The label broke this month. SoftVTBench built 4,000 expert demonstrations with FEM state held back as independent ground truth and found deformation-tolerance violations hiding inside recorded successes in all twelve in-distribution configurations tested, between 0.7% and 24% each ([August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html)). A resilience-metrics paper evaluating 400 household tasks across 10 embodied systems found recovery-cost differences of 25.2 among episodes that all counted as successes ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). InstructMove argued most manipulation benchmarks are contaminated because the target object is usually visually salient or uniquely feasible, so a policy scores without reading the instruction at all. None of these came from a vendor. Binary success being unsafe is now a published finding rather than a sales line, and process-level labels are a higher-margin product than outcome labels because somebody has to define the process.

Failure data got priced twice inside four weeks. On August 2 it was a community gesture, with Levine boosting OopsieData and calling discarded failure clips the most abundant but underused resource in robot learning ([August 2](../days/2026-08-02.html)). By August 17 FACT out of UCSD had made failure a first-class training signal for world-action models, with performance improving as more failure data is added ([August 17](../days/2026-08-17.html)). A week later Beyond Imitation took real bimanual stack-cups from 40% to 90% and insert-wallet from 25% to 80% off failed rollouts with frozen behavior-cloning weights, where filtered supervised fine-tuning stalled at 55% and 30% ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). And by August 28 LM-X disclosed a 20,000-hour training budget with over 1,000 hours of deliberately retained failed rollouts inside it ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Four weeks from trash to a line item in a frontier training run. Any collection contract that specifies only clean takes is now leaving money on the table.

Provenance stopped being a differentiator and became three separate hard requirements. Article 10 of the EU AI Act went enforceable on August 2 for high-risk systems, requiring documented data governance covering collection origin and every preparation step, with annotation and labelling named explicitly and compliance demonstrable to authorities on request ([August 2](../days/2026-08-02.html)). That is the first jurisdiction to write it into law, and buyer specs follow regulation across borders. Then TrapVLA demonstrated a backdoor where the attacker controls how the robot fails rather than merely that it fails, which turns poisoned demonstrations into a supply-chain security problem and provenance attestation into a product ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). And Rethinking Demonstration Unlearning showed the contributor-deletion question has no off-the-shelf answer, because behavior repair and membership evidence dissociate in both directions ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). Anyone selling human-collected ego data will meet all three of these contractually.

The vendor market moved in two directions at once. iMerit disappeared into EXL for up to $310M, $170M upfront plus $140M of earnouts, taking the Ango Hub platform and the Scholars network with it ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). That is the comp and the exit path for mid-tier annotation, and it removes an independent physical-AI annotation buyer from the field. Scale AI installed Francis deSouza, ex-Illumina and ex-Google Cloud, and the announcement led with BP, Mayo Clinic and government work rather than frontier-lab labeling ([August 4](../days/2026-08-04.html)). At the other end Surge ran its Tuesday Work Index three times in ten days, which is a labeling vendor turning itself into an evaluation authority, and Toloka published a named Shopify deployment on the judge-triages-then-humans-fix-the-tail architecture that robot rollout validation is converging on independently ([August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html)). The middle got squeezed and the top repositioned upstack.

The hardest TAM number of the month is in a prospectus. Leju earmarked roughly RMB 616M of a RMB 2.6B ChiNext raise specifically for high-quality large-scale datasets, at a RMB 4.327B post-investment valuation, while its own gross margin fell from 50.45% to 40.78% ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). A public company with a data budget line item, filed, while its robot margins compress. Leju has also invested in two training-data companies directly, and the same coverage counts at least 29 domestic Chinese embodied-intelligence companies now making external investments of their own. Robot makers buying equity in their own data suppliers is a different market structure from the western one where labs buy data on contract, and it showed up again with Kaiwang Data taking RMB 100M+ with Songyan and AgiBot's Mifeng on the cap table ([August 6](../days/2026-08-06.html)).

### Embedded AI systems

The genuine month-over-month change here is that the world-model inference-cost objection got dismantled. In July a world-action model was the heaviest thing anyone had proposed putting on a robot. Through August the field converged on keeping the benefit without paying the cost, from six directions at once. Faster-WAM found a single-layer action head matches deep designs at 3.2x the speed. RIFT skipped iterative video rollout entirely by consuming a fixed future K/V cache, cutting action-chunk latency 68 to 89%. ForeTime-VLA distilled the world-model teacher into 64-dimensional future tokens so the world model never runs at deployment at all. LAWA dropped the future-video branch at inference for 42.9% lower latency. RP1 cut rollout budget by three orders of magnitude ([August 6](../days/2026-08-06.html), [August 17](../days/2026-08-17.html), [August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html), [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Momenta's R7 world model reached a production Cadillac in the same month ([August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html)). The research-to-inference-budget gap is closing faster than the deployment evidence is arriving.

The small-model counterattack got extreme enough that we should carry it honestly. PredVLA runs 0.68 million trainable parameters, no robot-data pretraining at all, and reaches 86.9% mean success across the three short-horizon LIBERO suites, at 3.7x and 7.4x the mean success of parameter-matched Transformer and LSTM policies under identical protocol ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). That is a direct argument against the volume pitch, buyers will find it, and it is better that they find it with us than at us.

The result to actually take into our hardware spec is GS-VLA. A small displacement of the camera mount drops LIBERO success from about 90% to about 10% in the worst case, fixed by a 4M-parameter canonicalizer prepended to a frozen policy ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). Mount tolerance is a data-quality specification and not a mechanical detail, and that sentence belongs in our capture contract. EATR-Stereo is the other one, because it treats head-mounted stereo on a 33-DoF humanoid as a first-class interface rather than a nuisance to be corrected for ([August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html)).

On silicon the news is regional rather than technical. NVIDIA announced Jetson Orin Nano 2 at 78 TOPS with availability in the first half of 2027, so any bill of materials built on it this year is built on a promise ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). More useful is where the incumbents placed their bets. Renesas opened a Physical AI and Robotics Lab in Beijing and said it can address roughly 30% of the humanoid bill of materials today with an ambition of 70%. Qualcomm anchored a robotics investment initiative in Japan. Neither picked the US. On the Chinese side Axera posted H1 revenue up 181.8% with an embodied brain controller already in mass production, SiEngine began mass deliveries of TianGong 100, and Black Sesame signed into Midea's chip platform after signing Striding.AI on August 1 ([August 12](../days/2026-08-12.html), [August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html), [August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html)).

### Robotics & humanoids

This is the theme with the real reversal. Unitree priced at 150.80 yuan, raised RMB 6.1B against a RMB 4.2B target, closed day one up roughly 460% near RMB 342B, touched RMB 444.9B intraday, and then gave back close to RMB 200B of market capitalization in three sessions to sit near RMB 240B ([August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html), [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Even after halving that is above 140x price-to-sales on 2025 revenue of RMB 1.699B. Buried under the price action is the number that matters more: Q1 2026 revenue growth decelerated from 332.64% to 68.49%. Humanoid demand is order-book driven rather than compounding, and the prospectus said so before a single share traded, with adjusted net profit guided down 6.4% to 22% and humanoid ASP already down 72% since 2023 ([August 1](../days/2026-08-01.html), [August 5](../days/2026-08-05.html)). Then Wang Xingxing used his own WRC stage to say the ChatGPT moment for embodied AI is two to three years away at best and possibly a decade, and that humanoids are not ready to scale and still are not working in factories. The founder of the most-hyped company in the category talking his timeline down while his stock halves is the sentiment marker of the month.

The same repricing hit the West. SoftBank is in talks to buy a majority stake in 1X at roughly $6B against a reported $10B ask last year, which is a down-mark and a control transaction on the leading OpenAI-backed consumer humanoid, with NEO not yet in a customer's hands ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Money that did move went to capability and to data. Generalist stepped from $2B to $3B in about two months on a $200M extension led by 8VC, within a week of shipping GEN-1.5, which learns a new manipulation task from 3 to 12 seconds of a single demonstration with zero gradient updates and 59% average success across ten tasks ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html), [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). XPeng's Dogotix raised over $900M at a post above $6.3B with IDG leading and Tencent and Alibaba strategic, and XPeng's stated reason for volume deployment is generating real-world and human-demonstration data rather than selling robots.

The capability curve split visibly, and the split is the most useful thing to carry forward. Locomotion went vertical. Two humanoids beat Usain Bolt's 100m record inside 48 hours, Honor's Lightning at 9.32s and X-Humanoid's Tien Kung Ultra at 9.39s, and the year-over-year games delta is 21.50s to 9.32s on the 100m and 6:34 to 2:21 on the 1500m ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html), [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Manipulation did not. Robots at the same games failed to lift 33 lbs, UBTECH's own January disclosure that Walker S2 is at most half as efficient as a human worker still stands, and the Tien Kung engineering lead cautioned publicly that peak performance is not consistent reliability, naming structural fatigue and joint cooling. Legs are close to solved. Hands are not, and hands are where captured contact data goes.

Deployment revenue kept going to things that are not humanoids. HII signed up to $900M with Path Robotics and GrayMatter for autonomous welding in shipbuilding, Hadrian raised $1.37B at $7.87B for automated defense and aerospace factories, Bedrock has fully operator-free excavators on paying job sites in Nevada and Texas, and Gatik raised $200M with over $600M of contracted revenue and 85,000 completed driverless orders ([August 10](../days/2026-08-10.html), [August 11](../days/2026-08-11.html), [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Operator-free on a customer's site is a much higher bar than a 9.32s sprint and it got a fraction of the attention.

Two structural notes. First, carmakers keep building in-house rather than buying: BYD, Chery, FAW, GAC, XPeng and Xiaomi all now have their own humanoid programs, and Xiaomi published a real number from a four-month plant internship, nut-tightening from 90.2% to 98% ([August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html), [August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). Captive factory demand is the default first customer and that compresses the merchant humanoid market. Second, the shipment figures do not reconcile. A WRC industry report says China shipped more than 40,000 humanoids in H1, Smart Analytics Global says global H1 shipments were about 19,100, and Humanoids Daily says over 22,000, all claiming roughly 97% China share ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). These cannot all be true. Anyone quoting a humanoid TAM off these numbers is building on sand, and that includes decks we will be shown.

The cost curve underneath all of it is the underrated story. Average manufacturing cost of a general-purpose humanoid in 2026 is one-third of its 2023 level. Eyou holds 1.5 million integrated joint orders for 2026, fifteen times its entire 2025 shipments, off a line producing a harmonic joint every 60 seconds at 98% pass rate. Link-touch opened China's first fully automated force-sensor production line. Schaeffler will mass-produce formed strain wave gearboxes in Germany in 2027 at more than 25% lower cost ([August 17](../days/2026-08-17.html), [August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html), [August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). Bodies are getting cheap fast, which is precisely what makes data the binding constraint from here.

### India & SEA spotlight

India finally produced a demand number. Addverb, Reliance-controlled, trains on 1,000 hours of data a week today and plans to reach 5,000, three years into its physical-AI effort, targeting industrial and defence ([August 19](../days/2026-08-19.html)). Read that as a domestic buyer with a published appetite of 4,000 incremental hours a week. Every prior India datapoint in this brief has been supply-side or aspirational. This one is a buyer, with a number, in print. The same Business Standard piece carries Ati Robotics making actuators in India, over 50% of the bill of materials, while siting final assembly in Michigan for the US market, which is a specific engineered answer to the tariff and security regime rather than a complaint about it.

The state started writing checks. ideaForge took Rs 151 crore from the RDI scheme, among the first companies funded from the Rs 1 lakh crore corpus, and the first one went to autonomy ([August 10](../days/2026-08-10.html)). Entrackr now tracks Indian Physical AI as its own line, at roughly $155M across 31 deals in 2026, already about 19% above the full-year 2025 total ([August 15](../days/2026-08-15.html)). Set that against China's 93.5B yuan of H1 embodied financing across 322 deals and $47.3B of global physical-AI venture funding in H1, and the ratio is what it is. The other named Indian rounds stayed off-thesis in the useful sense, funding infrastructure verticals with paying customers: Solinas at $5.5M for sewer inspection robots, AlgoFET at Rs 15 crore with 2,000+ units deployed and an order book near Rs 100 crore, NeoGeo at $20M for geospatial acquisition ([August 11](../days/2026-08-11.html), [August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html)). WATER Robotics is the one worth watching, at $2.5M for a robotic bed carrying 10,752 pressure sensors, which in a hospital is a continuous body-contact capture rig that happens to also be furniture ([August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)).

The gap week is the honest note. Between August 20 and 24 India allocated zero disclosed dollars to robotics while China ran two national robotics events, set two world records and put a humanoid into Mercedes-Benz dealerships ([August 24](../days/2026-08-24.html)). And China is now arriving in the neighbourhood rather than staying home. Maimai has secured overseas orders for 20,000 units this year with an ASEAN headquarters in Malaysia and rubber, durian and palm named as the agriculture wedge. Linkhou started production at its first Vietnam plant. Chery's AiMOGA named SEA and the Middle East as priority markets for humanoid police robots off 3,000+ delivered globally ([August 6](../days/2026-08-06.html), [August 20](../days/2026-08-20.html), [August 28](../days/2026-08-28.html)). Singapore stayed the region's counter-example, with Ropedia shipping its fourth capture rig in twelve months and its co-founder arguing publicly that fixed cameras and controlled demonstrations cannot produce in-the-wild diversity, which is our own framing said back to us by somebody who is funded and shipping.

## Papers added this month

236 entries went into the Papers DB in August, more than the previous three months combined. The ones that move our thesis:

- [Dyna-2](https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2) (Dyna Robotics). 1M+ hours of egocentric human video and a published human-to-robot scaling law across four orders of magnitude, with transfer to zero-shot evaluation on unseen robot data. Numbers carry a publisher correction. The single most important release of the month for us.
- [SoftVTBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18701). FEM ground truth held back as evaluator-only, and 0.7% to 24% of recorded successes violated deformation tolerance in every configuration. Binary success is not a safe label.
- [SiMDex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04196) (U Tokyo). Mining under 5% of a 32M-sample ego pool lifts real success from 47.7% to 61.1% against equal-size random. Curation beats volume, on hard numbers.
- [GS-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19066). A small camera-mount displacement drops LIBERO success from about 90% to about 10%. Mount tolerance is a data-quality spec.
- [Beyond Imitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21204). Real bimanual 40% to 90% and 25% to 80% off failed rollouts with frozen BC weights, where filtered SFT stalls at 55% and 30%. Failure data priced.
- [FACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10232) (UCSD). Failure as a first-class training signal for world-action models, with performance improving as more failure data is added.
- [Riemann-1.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27033). 200,000+ hours unifying egocentric human video, handheld-gripper demonstrations and robot trajectories under one objective, no retargeting stage.
- [UCAG-P](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26058). 4.03K hours of robot and sim data plus 2.34K hours of human demonstrations in one camera-centric schema. Human hours are more than a third of a frontier-grade pretraining mix.
- [Ego2Robot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02580) and [RoboEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18948). 18,561 hours across 15 morphologies, and 174K aligned pairs across 14M frames from plain RGB with no mocap and no teleop. The conversion layer is published method now.
- [Ego-OSCAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08285). A sub-$200 open-source head-mounted stereo rig with 550 crowdsourced hours attached. The capture-rig floor price.
- [TrapVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26578). A backdoor where the attacker specifies how the robot fails. Provenance becomes a security control.
- [Rethinking Demonstration Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20784). Behavior repair and membership evidence dissociate in both directions, so the contributor-deletion clause has no off-the-shelf answer.
- [ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17386). LTLf automaton monitors over physics-grounded predicates instead of learned classifiers or LLM judges, with a safety-annotated trajectory pipeline. Formal specification as annotation schema.
- [PRM-as-a-Judge 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14284) (BAAI orbit). Dense progress curves and fine-grained quality metrics beyond binary success, plus a testbed for evaluating the judges themselves.
- [GEN-1.5](https://generalistai.com/blog/gen-1.5) (Generalist AI). One-shot in-context skill acquisition from 3 to 12 seconds of a single demonstration, 59% across ten tasks with zero gradient updates.
- [Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA-Data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tencent/Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA-Data) (Tencent). 10,000+ hours of UMI human demonstrations, ~1M episodes, Apache-2.0, with the capture-to-RL pipeline. What it does not release is the QC that decided which hours made the cut.
- [PhysicalAI-Robotics-Open-H-Embodiment](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/PhysicalAI-Robotics-Open-H-Embodiment) (NVIDIA). 750 hours from 33 organizations, labeled hybrid across human, automatic sensor and synthetic. Human validation stays in the loop at frontier scale.
- [PredVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26673). 0.68M parameters, no robot pretraining, 86.9% on short-horizon LIBERO. Read it as the counter-argument to our own deck.
- [The Missing Touch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19372). Spatially distributed tactile feedback cuts teleop-to-human trajectory deviation by 29% to 79%. Rig quality determines dataset quality, measured.
- [Isaac 0.5](https://huggingface.co/PerceptronAI/Isaac-0.5) (Perceptron). An ex-FAIR team naming GoPro and wearable first-person task capture in a disclosed open-weight training mix. Third-party validation with no commercial stake in our answer.

## What to watch in September

- Addverb's 4,000 incremental hours a week. That is the only named Indian buyer with a published number, and the question for September is whether anybody signs against it. Mech-Mind also starts trading September 1 under 09615 at 64% gross margin, one week after the robot-body company halved, which is the picks-and-shovels thesis getting a live public-market test.
- Whether Dyna-2's human-to-robot scaling law survives replication. It is the most valuable claim of the month for a capture business and it shipped with a publisher correction attached. If an independent group reproduces the curve, hours become a priced input with an elasticity. If it does not reproduce, we go back to arguing coverage.
- The listing queue behind Unitree, now that the comp has halved. RobotEra targeting $800M-$1B in Hong Kong, RealMan on ChiNext, Leju on ChiNext with an RMB 616M data line item in its prospectus, AgiBot still in process, and AI² Robotics reportedly eyeing 2027. Watch whether any of them cuts its target, and watch Leju's data line specifically because it is the closest thing to a public price for our product.
- GenRobot's open-sourcing strategy. Giving away 13,000+ hours to own the format, at over a million downloads a month, is a play we do not currently have an answer to. If SYNAPATH or INFIFORCE copy it, the floor price for undifferentiated hours goes to zero faster than we planned for.
- Whether the EU Article 10 obligations start showing up in actual buyer specs rather than legal commentary. The regulation went live August 2. The interesting signal is the first robotics procurement document that quotes it, because that is the moment audit trails stop being something we explain and start being something buyers ask for by name.
