    September 2026 review

    September strengthened the case for egocentric demonstrations while making their limits clearer. Human-to-robot transfer studies increasingly tested alignment, task variation, contact sensing and downstream policy outcomes. Data marketplaces and supplier invitations offered routes to buyers, but rarely disclosed firm orders or usable vendor economics. Large proposed acquisitions reached across model hosting, world models and perception. Deployment announcements became more specific about workflows and safety, although laboratory success, planned fleets and commercial uptime remained different measures.

    Coverage is sparse: 8 daily inputs out of 30; 22 missing days. Saved editions: 7, 8, 10, 23, 24, 25, 26 and 30. Missing: September 1–6, 9, 11–22 and 27–29. Later catch-ups recover some events, not complete daily coverage. This is an archive synthesis, not an exhaustive month-long search. Public Reddit/X and institutional-source access were partial. AMD, Dyna and Destro are explicitly dated September supplements verified in the October 1 daily; they do not increase the input-day count.

    TL;DR

    • Human video is becoming a more differentiated training input. XPACE separates heterogeneous experience and aligned bridge data; Figure’s Helix 2.5 evaluation reports better performance in unseen homes, without claiming unseen-task generalisation. Collection proposals should specify the training role and a buyer-agreed evaluation.
    • The platform deals are agreements, not verified completed ownership changes. NVIDIA announced its approximately $12.93B Hugging Face agreement on September 3. AMD announced an approximately $8.2B World Labs agreement on September 28. Watch approvals, closing and access terms.
    • Business intake exists, but an open route is not an order. DeepReach, Praxis and Defined.ai are distinct routes. India-specific vendor requests surfaced too, mostly with unresolved dates, availability and acceptance/payment terms.
    • Quality needs to survive the control loop. LIBERO-VPro, VLAQuantBench and recovery benchmarks test weaknesses hidden by clean scenes or isolated inference scores. Sensor timing, failure coverage and rejected footage belong in collection design.
    • October’s commercial watchpoints are concrete: qualify current supplier orders, verify proposed transactions, follow October contributor studies without treating their pay as vendor rates, and require deployment evidence beyond announced fleet sizes. The appendix inventories 158 library entries added in September, including older research.

    Biggest moves by theme

    Data collection

    The stronger argument was about useful experience, not hours alone. XPACE’s September 15 paper combines general human experience, action-aligned data, bridging and robot experience; it distinguishes world-model failure learning from imitation. Figure’s September 17 study reports whole-task success rising from 9% to 56% with Index pretraining under its comparison conditions across 30 unseen homes. These are company/research results, not a guarantee that any additional video produces the same return. XPACE, Figure.

    The late-month papers broadened capture choices. DexRoam uses whole-body human demonstrations for mobile manipulation; MonoEgo studies metric monocular capture with wrist markers and workstation anchors; PolyUMI, VisTacAlign and visual-tactile-action modelling make contact sensing a deliberate part of demonstrations. Their hardware and tasks differ. They do not establish one universal capture rig or imply that tactile instrumentation is already available to every supplier.

    Two releases also illustrated why dataset totals need qualification. Gen-HumanEgo’s September 23 card reports 1,847.7 hours, six cameras and structured supervision, but some labels are model-derived and task counts differ between the card and launch materials. Eidon ceased operating and released archived assets; its tracker-POV card reports 1,273.8 hours from 27 contributors, with substantial imbalance and incomplete raw-motion coverage. Availability of a download does not settle consent, privacy or fitness for a particular use. Gen-HumanEgo card, Eidon archive, tracker-POV card.

    Marketplaces became more visible through Kinetic Blocks and the September 21 Deepen Refinery announcement. These offer distribution, licensing and validation mechanisms; the archive does not verify realised sales or standing collection orders. Nor does Eidon’s closure establish a cause or prove that the entire human-video market is failing. For an egocentric collection business, the proposed decision is to describe tasks, sites, sensor provenance, permitted uses and acceptance criteria before pricing a generic hour of footage. Kinetic Blocks coverage, with publisher affiliation disclosed, Deepen supplier waitlist.

    Data annotation & validation

    Evaluation moved closer to operational failure. CALIPER questions what clean scenes reveal about physical inference. LIBERO-RECOVER and RoboRecover probe recovery, while LIBERO-VPro tests visual disruption in closed-loop control. VLAQuantBench similarly evaluates compressed models as policies, rather than inferring control quality from model size or latency. Together they support a collection specification that preserves timing, interventions, failure episodes and task boundaries. They do not provide interchangeable benchmark scores. CALIPER, LIBERO-RECOVER, RoboRecover, VLAQuantBench.

    The September 16 EMG-force audit adds a practical warning: channel names alone do not establish direct force measurements or reliable alignment. Synthetic and inferred supervision can be useful, but provenance should remain visible. Scale’s READY and verifier work, Mercor’s RL guide and Snorkel’s September 22 funding belong to the wider AI-data/evaluation market; none proves a corresponding open robotics collection budget. READY, Snorkel.

    Correction to the September 10 archive: AWS confirms Mechanical Turk’s September 30 permanent closure. Its notices distinguish that event from Ground Truth and Augmented AI restrictions on new customers starting July 30, and Ground Truth Plus ending June 30. The older blanket claim that all three services close September 30 should not be carried forward. EXL’s iMerit agreement was announced June 24; its later appearance in a September brief does not make it September deal flow. MTurk notice, AWS feature notices, EXL original agreement, September 10 historical brief.

    Embedded AI systems

    Software availability and control-aware efficiency were the useful changes. September 22 brought Isaac ROS 5.0 and Intrinsic Core, including an open machine-tending reference. Universal Robots announced its Gen 7 platform on September 14. These expand development choices; they are not evidence that integration, calibration or operational support have disappeared. Gen 7.

    Research on adaptive action chunking, early exits, one-step generation and split inference asks where computation can be removed without harming control. Decoupled Early Exits and VLAQuantBench make this a task-dependent question. PrismML’s September 23 smart-glasses demonstration is a specific model/device result, not verified all-day battery performance. RoboSense’s September 28 Holoscan Sensor Bridge integration and Innodisk’s September 14 multi-camera platform highlight sensor transport and synchronisation alongside inference. PrismML, RoboSense, Innodisk.

    Robotics & humanoids

    Workflows became clearer, but maturity still varied. Agility announced Digit 5 on September 15 with future availability, Boston Dynamics opened its Robotics Metaplant Application Center on September 21, and FANUC’s September 11 AI Welding Agent announcement described a later shipping schedule. AGIBOT reported a first phase of more than 300 robots with Chimelong on September 24. A company-reported count, a training centre and a product announcement measure different things; none substitutes for independent uptime, intervention or economic data. Digit 5, Boston Dynamics, FANUC, AGIBOT.

    September 29 supplement, verified October 1: Dyna 2.1 combines a mobile dual-arm platform, a unified robot representation and a slower orchestration layer for extended workflows such as laundry. Its published material explains the architecture and company demonstrations. It does not supply independently audited fleet reliability; its failure-rate calculator is illustrative. Dyna technical release.

    World-model research also became more cautious about control. InternW0, PointCast and Rolling-WAM explore different representations and rollout strategies; When World Models Lie examines safety under incorrect predictions. Improved generated video is not automatically improved planning or safe execution. The archive also added February’s EgoScale and DreamDojo as belated background; those are not September releases. InternW0, PointCast, Rolling-WAM, When World Models Lie.

    India & SEA spotlight

    The evidence spans engineering, operating partners and adjacent capture demand. Jaipur Robotics’ €4.3M seed round concerns a Swiss-headquartered company with Bengaluru engineering. Hivebotics’ $6M Series A supports its Singapore cleaning-robot business. Neither should be presented as a census of all regional funding. IISc/ARTPARK’s noise-robust speech release is relevant to multilingual collection and labelling, while remaining separate from egocentric manipulation data. Jaipur, Hivebotics, ARTPARK.

    NVIDIA’s September 22 regional update describes NCS/HTX work in Singapore, ITMAX traffic applications in Malaysia and AS-TECH passenger-flow work in Thailand. These are programmes at different stages, not a verified regional humanoid deployment total. IFR’s September 24 report records almost 10,500 Indian industrial-robot installations in 2025, up 15%; it measures factory automation, not a data-procurement market. Regional update, IFR.

    The actionable September discovery was a set of India vendor/workplace invitations and October speech-study specifications, detailed below. DeepReach’s specific regional business call lists five SEA countries and does not establish Indian eligibility. Thin institutional coverage cannot support the older briefs’ broad assertions that India had no relevant activity.

    China spotlight

    Capital, domestic computing and industrial scale all mattered. D-Robotics’ reported $400M Series C and SemiDrive’s nearly RMB1B across three 2026 rounds concern infrastructure; the latter is a combined-year update, not a new RMB1B round on September 21. Reports on InstAdapt and LingYu also point to collection and simulation infrastructure. Sharpa’s Singapore registration and founder links make a simple country label inadequate. D-Robotics, SemiDrive, Sharpa background.

    IFR reports 354,000 industrial robots installed in China in 2025, representing 59% of global installations. Domestic suppliers’ unit volume rose while their market share moved from 57% to 55%. This is industrial-robot evidence, not a count of autonomous humanoids or collection customers. Research including XPACE and InternW0 adds technical context; papers and financing do not by themselves validate advertised deployments. IFR.

    Across regions, public X coverage was incomplete. Founder commentary about buyer concentration or data quality remains opinion unless backed by disclosed transactions or controlled results. No account’s absence from accessible search results is treated as evidence that it was silent.
