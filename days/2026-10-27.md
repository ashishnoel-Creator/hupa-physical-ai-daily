### Data collection

**Human video is becoming a richer pretraining source.** HuGe combines over 2,000 hours of human and robot experience, using simulation-derived pseudo-proprioception and visual adaptation. RynnVLA-Latent converts **30.1K hours of mixed human, robot and simulated video** into latent-action supervision; that figure is not an order for human footage. EXE separates transferable manipulation structure from human-specific motion. These papers strengthen the case for diverse task coverage while showing that plain RGB still needs transformation before it supervises robot control. [HuGe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34186) · [RynnVLA-Latent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34356) · [EXE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33685)

**Capture beyond RGB.** DEX-PHD logs wrist video, proprioception and fingertip contact from an untethered dexterous handheld device. Its demonstration is open-loop humanoid replay, so learned-policy generalization remains unestablished. FLORE uses force-sensing handheld grippers and body trackers; its three-task evaluation reports 80% success versus 26.7% for position-only control on the same demonstrations. Hardware, calibration and synchronized contact signals are substantive additions to an ordinary headcam workflow. [DEX-PHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35462) · [FLORE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35862)

**Automation changes the labour mix.** AuRA uses robot interaction to acquire deformable digital twins, reporting lower active human labour across 13 objects. Zero-HLM uses smartphone scene capture and simulated demonstrations without real robot demonstrations. Neither removes the need to acquire and validate the physical scene. [AuRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34452) · [Zero-HLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35075)

### Data annotation & validation

**Outcome judges need their own audits.** RoboJev's open success judge reports strong held-out results, but its unseen-lab AUROC is slightly below the base model, and its frontier comparisons use different frame counts. A separate experiment finds that false execution logs can sway visual progress judgments. Keep video evidence, operator narration and machine logs distinguishable in QA. [RoboJev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33718) · [Execution-log study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34151)

**Success is insufficient.** WorldFailBench tests where and why policies fail; RoboWorM checks kinematic and multiview consistency. SPECOLA measures specification margins and persistence, while EAST finds successful rollouts that violate safety requirements. The learning-utility study independently warns that realistic generated human behaviour need not be the best training partner. These are research findings, not universally validated acceptance standards. [WorldFailBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33565) · [RoboWorM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33807) · [SPECOLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35730) · [EAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34358) · [Utility study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34839)

### Embedded AI systems

**VLA-HWBench profiles the actual bottlenecks.** Eighteen models on Jetson AGX Thor and AMD Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 395 show large latency differences driven by action generation and software. Parameter count alone is a poor latency proxy. TRIAGE chooses cached reuse versus pruning using token drift; TinyFlow removes a resampling bottleneck for static INT8 execution. Thermal-Headroom Routing is more preliminary: its throttling improvements are simulated, with hardware measurements supporting only part of the model. [VLA-HWBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34717) · [TRIAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34519) · [TinyFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33338) · [Thermal study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35231)
