### Data collection

**September 2026 writeup, missed catch-up: Dream Machines tests the composition of a manufacturing dataset.** On bimanual actuator unboxing and tray insertion, the team collected 7,271 episodes, about 21 hours, with two operators and three cameras. A four-hour set spanning five scenes outperformed a four-hour single-scene set by 30 percentage points. Adding one deliberately clean hour to the 21-hour baseline raised reported success from 76% to 90%. Most comparisons use 40 rollouts, with wide confidence intervals; this is one task, not a universal return per collected hour. [Technical writeup](https://dream-machines.eu/blog/pi05-fine-tuning)

The released high-quality subset has **198 episodes**, 50 Hz state/action data and three camera views. Its LeRobot v3 export crops the original 640×480 images to 224×224. That distinction matters when specifying capture versus training-delivery resolution. Exact dataset publication day was not established. [Dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/DreamMachines/actuator_unboxing_1h_HQ)

**September 24 preprint, missed catch-up: PROPRA makes a case for synchronised contact data.** It aligns fingertip pressure and reflective-proximity histories with proprioception and actions. The authors find that simply adding sensors can underperform vision alone; phase-aware pretraining improves their real manipulation experiments. The collection implication is to preserve alignment and pre-contact context, rather than assume more modalities automatically improve a policy. The abstract provides no single numerical gain. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29822)

### Data annotation & validation

**September 18, missed catch-up: RoboHarm separates refusal, failure and task completion.** Its published study contains 300 trials: 100 each for Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and MolmoAct2, across five fixed instructions on one bimanual bench. Reported safety refusals were 20, 2 and 0 respectively; harmful-task completions were 34, 60 and 6. Low completion is not equivalent to a safety refusal, particularly for a policy without a semantic refusal mechanism. Human-reviewed videos/transcripts support this small test, not an estimate of real-world injury risk. Useful evaluation labels must distinguish these outcomes instead of collapsing them into success/failure. [Report and results](https://robocurve.org/roboharm/)

| Paper | Result and limit |
