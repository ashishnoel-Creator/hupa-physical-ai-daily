    Data collection

    September 2026 writeup, missed catch-up: Dream Machines tests the composition of a manufacturing dataset. On bimanual actuator unboxing and tray insertion, the team collected 7,271 episodes, about 21 hours, with two operators and three cameras. A four-hour set spanning five scenes outperformed a four-hour single-scene set by 30 percentage points. Adding one deliberately clean hour to the 21-hour baseline raised reported success from 76% to 90%. Most comparisons use 40 rollouts, with wide confidence intervals; this is one task, not a universal return per collected hour. Technical writeup

    The released high-quality subset has 198 episodes, 50 Hz state/action data and three camera views. Its LeRobot v3 export crops the original 640×480 images to 224×224. That distinction matters when specifying capture versus training-delivery resolution. Exact dataset publication day was not established. Dataset card

    September 24 preprint, missed catch-up: PROPRA makes a case for synchronised contact data. It aligns fingertip pressure and reflective-proximity histories with proprioception and actions. The authors find that simply adding sensors can underperform vision alone; phase-aware pretraining improves their real manipulation experiments. The collection implication is to preserve alignment and pre-contact context, rather than assume more modalities automatically improve a policy. The abstract provides no single numerical gain. Paper

    Data annotation & validation

    September 18, missed catch-up: RoboHarm separates refusal, failure and task completion. Its published study contains 300 trials: 100 each for Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and MolmoAct2, across five fixed instructions on one bimanual bench. Reported safety refusals were 20, 2 and 0 respectively; harmful-task completions were 34, 60 and 6. Low completion is not equivalent to a safety refusal, particularly for a policy without a semantic refusal mechanism. Human-reviewed videos/transcripts support this small test, not an estimate of real-world injury risk. Useful evaluation labels must distinguish these outcomes instead of collapsing them into success/failure. Report and results

    Research papers worth a look

    All six added arXiv papers were originally submitted September 24 and are missed catch-up from the latest listing, rather than September 25 submissions. PROPRA and Riemannian MeanFlow are discussed above; these four add distinct results:

    Paper Result and limit
    WorldActionAgent Combines visual reasoning, rehearsal and reusable skills with execution traces. Reports 75.6% LIBERO-Pro success; training Qwen3.5-9B on its traces raises out-of-domain success from 1.7% to 43.3%. These are benchmark results, not verified workplace autonomy.
    Underwater C3-JEPA Predicts object/contact evolution from synchronised multi-view RGB and ROV controls without contact sensors. Simulated experiments and held-out-camera prediction on real underwater video support the representation. They do not establish autonomous real salvage.
    Coding Agents for Generalized Task and Motion Planning Evaluates 980 programs over 98,000 held-out episodes in 28 simulated environments. On the 16 with planner baselines, agent configurations average 56–95% success versus 47% for planners. Physical deployment is not demonstrated.
    GPT-6-Astra embodied navigation evaluation Reports 79% zero-shot R2R-CE success using monocular RGB and ultra reasoning. Route execution and goal verification remain failure points. This is simulated navigation, not a hardware reliability claim.
