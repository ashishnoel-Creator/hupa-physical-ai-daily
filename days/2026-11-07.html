    Data collection

    November 6 · Physicl Studio targets failure-specific data. The company announced a $10M seed, led by Inovia with US Venture Partners participating. Studio offers 10,000 environments, 100,000+ assets and controlled scene variations; its current product page identifies the offering as private beta. Rendered outputs include metric depth, normals, object instances, boxes and camera poses. Its SpAItial reconstruction improvement is company-reported, without an independently reproduced protocol here. This is a synthetic-data product, not a request to purchase footage. Company release.

    Catch-up · November 4–5 capture research. EMG-guided bottle manipulation records human muscle activity as force references; Semantics-to-Contact uses human-in-the-loop residual RL and force feedback. Both show why RGB-only hours can miss task-relevant force information. MetricTrail improves streaming reconstruction with metric-depth constraints, while FREE-LIVO tests asynchronous, nonrigid LiDAR-camera setups. These methods do not remove a customer’s calibration, synchronization or sensor requirements.

    Data annotation & validation

    Catch-up · November 5: evaluate the annotations available at deployment. Egocentric Action Recognition reports 68.4% Top-1 with annotated active-object boxes versus 49.0% with detector boxes; affordance filtering reaches 49.9%. The important comparison is against realistic detections, not an oracle that already identifies the relevant object.

    THG-Omni links participant tracks to temporally grounded, checked semantic claims. VideoRouteAudit holds model and frame budgets fixed: none of its tested selectors significantly beats uniform sampling on the full 900-item long-video set. ReferClaw asks clarifying questions before answering ambiguous ego-video requests. Together these are useful evaluation directions: trace the supporting evidence, isolate the intervention and record unresolved intent.

    Whole-body calibration reports 0.50–1.11 mm mean held-out errors on two robots after jointly estimating waist/arms. XM4D supplies optimality certificates for its formulated geometry problems; those certificates do not certify every physical property of the reconstructed scene.
