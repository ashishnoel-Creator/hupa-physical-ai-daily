    TL;DR

    • The FCC banned imports of new Chinese-made humanoid and quadruped robots on July 28, citing supply chain and cybersecurity risk, and China’s foreign ministry fired back within a day. The ban is not retroactive, but it lands on a market where Unitree holds roughly a fifth of global humanoid share and Unitree plus AgiBot shipped over 10,000 of the ~15,000 humanoids sold worldwide in 2025. TechCrunch, CNN
    • BeingBeyond released Being-H0.8 on July 28, the first embodied model to pull tactile modalities into large-scale pre-training, using TactoHand to pseudo-label touch signals across 500,000+ hours of ordinary human video at near-zero marginal cost. If touch can be inferred from plain video at usable fidelity, the capture-rig sensor debate moves again toward what ground truth you verify against. Gasgoo
    • Delta Intelligence closed an Angel++ round of nearly RMB 500M, its sixth round in six months, and launched Delta D1, a head-mounted whole-body panoramic ego capture rig, in the same breath. The cap table is the story: humanoid OEMs AgiBot, Leju and Galaxea AI are all investors, which means the robot makers are now funding their own data-capture supply chain. Gasgoo

    Data collection

    • Delta D1 is the closest thing yet to a funded Chinese analog of the rig-first thesis. A head-mounted device worn during normal operations, no mocap studio or site retrofitting, recording first-person panoramic vision and whole-body joint trajectories, with the output described as training-ready immediately after collection. A handheld D1-Gripper accessory captures end-effector detail for fine dual-arm tasks. Delta was spun out of the Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence in January 2026 and has model deployment partnerships with Zhiyuan (AgiBot), Leju, Galaxea and Unitree. Six rounds in six months, backers including Hillhouse, Fosun RZ, Lenovo Capital and three humanoid OEMs, and the round proceeds explicitly earmarked for mass production of collection devices and a data closed loop. The lane keeps getting funded: Ropedia in Singapore three weeks ago, Delta in Beijing now. Neither claims audit-trailed validation against a buyer spec. Gasgoo
    • Being-H0.8’s TactoHand system supplements massive volumes of ordinary human video with tactile supervision without gloves or sensors, inferring contact probability and continuous proximity at dense spatial points. BeingBeyond says it has extended tactile labels to 500,000+ hours of first-person video this way, and its TopoHand action space gives human hands, dexterous hands and parallel grippers one unified kinematic interface. Same direction as last week’s FELT paper, but at pre-training scale and from a company with dozens of named data collaborators. Every sensor a pipeline can infer from pixels is a sensor a capture spec no longer has to carry, and the paid work moves further toward verifying the inference. Gasgoo
    • HiFi-UMI (arXiv 2607.25895, submitted July 29) trains deployable manipulation policies from high-fidelity handheld UMI data alone, no teleoperation, and ships the HiFi-UMI-2K dataset openly on Hugging Face. The cheap-capture-hardware lineage keeps compounding: if handheld gripper data alone gets policies to deployment, the value concentrates in how carefully the capture was run, which is an operations argument. arXiv
    • TechCrunch ran a feature asking whether brain waves are the next unlock for physical AI, built around operators generating robot training data on leader-follower rigs. Filed as a pointer: neural and EMG signals as a capture modality is a thread worth watching, not yet a market. TechCrunch

    Data annotation & validation

    • Gasgoo published the sharpest practitioner synthesis yet on why the data bottleneck is not scale: quality imbalance, fragmented standards, and a missing mid-pipeline. The named specifics matter. XYZ Embodied AI tried training on 200,000+ datapoints mixed from 20+ robot brands and got an unusable mess until it built its own standardization platform. Manifold AI’s CEO says embodied collection is all front-end acquisition while the cleaning, labeling and QC stages that autonomous driving pipelines treat as mandatory are simply missing. XYZ discloses a 90/10 split, non-robot data for pre-training and real-robot data for post-training alignment. JD Logistics argues failure and recovery data is severely underpriced, and that warehouse WMS records (weight, dimensions, material) are ready-made labels nobody joins to visual data. The piece also sorts the market into four player types, with third-party data services framed as the industry’s utilities layer. Read the whole thing. It is the demand spec for a validation business, written by the buyers. Gasgoo
    • Data Pyramid for Embodied Manipulation (arXiv 2607.24744, submitted July 28) is a large multi-institution survey that codifies the internet-video / human-capture / robot-teleop pyramid the industry keeps describing informally, with a maintained repo of datasets at each layer. Useful as the citable reference for where ego capture sits in the stack and what annotation each layer is expected to carry. arXiv
    • The named vendors (Scale, Mercor, Surge, Labelbox, Snorkel, Sama, iMerit, Invisible, Centific, Toloka) published nothing dated this window.

    Embedded AI systems

    • LightWheel and Huixi Technology signed a strategic partnership on July 28 pairing LightWheel’s human-behavior data platform with Huixi’s 500 TOPS Guangzhi R1 chip and RISE edge computing platform. The stated plan is a full chain from data generation through edge processing to on-site deployment feedback, with failure cases, intervention logs and environmental shifts recorded in the field fed back into data production to retrain. First targets: industrial manufacturing, warehousing, commercial services. This is the capture-to-edge-to-correction loop as a productized reference stack, from a Chinese pairing. Gasgoo
    • Lumos Robotics launched Lumos Station Lite on July 27, an all-in-one platform for deploying, executing and quantitatively evaluating embodied models on physical robots, aimed at universities and corporate R&D. Context that never made a brief here: Lumos raised nearly RMB 1B across A1 and A2 rounds in May, led by Mitsubishi Electric. A Japanese industrial giant leading a billion-yuan round into Chinese embodied-AI infrastructure is its own signal. Gasgoo

    Robotics & humanoids

    • The FCC ban is the biggest structural move in the humanoid market this year. What it covers: new Chinese-made humanoid and quadruped robots plus network-connected power inverters, on supply chain and cybersecurity grounds, with the FCC citing remote control and surveillance risk. What it does not cover: anything already through FCC equipment authorization, so existing Unitree and AgiBot models keep selling in the US. The exposure math: Unitree holds nearly a fifth of the global humanoid market, and Unitree and AgiBot each shipped more than 5,000 of the roughly 15,000 humanoids sold globally in 2025. It also lands 48 hours after AgiBot formally started its Hong Kong IPO and while Unitree’s approved STAR listing sits unpriced, so both prospectuses now need a US-market risk rewrite. China’s foreign ministry responded on July 29 that it firmly opposes the move and will take necessary measures. For every Western robot program, the second-order effect is a higher hardware floor: the cheapest capable platforms just got fenced out of the largest buyer market. TechCrunch, NPR, Gasgoo
    • WanJiYiZu won a public bid to lease humanoid robots to the DoubleTree by Hilton in Hengqin. Small item, notable mechanism: humanoids entering hospitality through competitive leasing tenders rather than pilots or PR deployments. Procurement process is a maturity signal. Gasgoo

    China spotlight

    • The China read on the FCC ban: it accelerates exactly the things that were already compounding. Domestic demand absorbs shipment volume (WAIC had 300+ physical robots on floor), the IPO capital comes from Hong Kong and Shanghai rather than New York, and the component stack was already localizing. The MFA statement promises necessary measures without naming any. Watch for export controls on rare-earth actuator components as the reply instrument. Gasgoo
    • Beyond the ban, the week’s Chinese items are all data-layer: Delta’s rig and round, Being-H0.8’s tactile pre-training, LightWheel-Huixi’s edge loop, Lumos’s eval station. Four announcements in three days, all of them about the pipeline between capture and deployment rather than about new robot bodies. The Chinese ecosystem has visibly rotated its announcement mix from humanoid reveals to data infrastructure. Sections above carry the detail.

    Research papers worth a look

    • Being-H0.8: implicit tactile world-action model (BeingBeyond, released July 28). Full read in the collection section above. Tactile modality in large-scale pre-training via TactoHand pseudo-labels over 500K+ hours of ego video, TopoHand unified action space, slow-fast action expert for high-frequency tactile feedback. Gasgoo
    • HiFi-UMI: Learning Deployable Manipulation Policies from High-Fidelity UMI Data Alone (arXiv 2607.25895, July 29). Full read in collection above. Includes the open HiFi-UMI-2K dataset. arXiv
    • Data Pyramid for Embodied Manipulation (arXiv 2607.24744, July 28). Full read in annotation above. The citable codification of the data pyramid, with a maintained dataset repo. arXiv
    • τ: Learning Touch-Augmented Vision-Language-Action Models from Future Visual Supervision (arXiv 2607.24485, July 28). Trains touch-augmented VLAs by supervising against future visual states instead of dedicated tactile ground truth. Second paper this window deriving touch without touch sensors; the trend is now unmistakable. arXiv
    • When Does Legacy Data Start to Help? Emergent Transfer in Cross-Configuration Robot Learning (arXiv 2607.25593, July 29). Asks when data collected on old robot configurations transfers to new ones and finds transfer emerges rather than accruing linearly. Directly relevant to dataset resale value: data that outlives the hardware it was captured on is worth more than the market prices it at today. arXiv
    • ArmnetBench v0.1 (arXiv 2607.24481, July 28). Parallel real-world evaluation of manipulation policies on a low-cost arm farm, data on Hugging Face. Cheap parallelized physical eval is the missing acceptance-testing infrastructure; every step it takes makes validated delivery more checkable. arXiv
