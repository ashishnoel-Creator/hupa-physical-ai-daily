**Catch-up, November 12/16: Ropedia Xperience 2.** The revised paper describes **1,589 metric-scale 3D scans**, **4,230 ego recordings**, **74 tasks** and **131 hours**. It proposes four reuse paths: interactive simulated scenes, retargeted human motion, virtual teleoperation and generated long-horizon trajectories. The authors report the scans as released; downloads and commercial-use licensing were not independently tested here. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17060)

**November 16: WinDEX and TaF-Foundation.** WinDEX uses calibrated stereo-inertial capture, metric hand reconstruction and model-assisted annotation, with 1,000 hours of training annotations and separate human/mocap evaluation sets. TaF-Foundation pairs more than 100 million tactile observations with measured wrenches across 24 sensors and four sensing families. Interpretation: useful collection specifications increasingly include calibration, temporal alignment and physical reference measurements, not only a target number of hours. [WinDEX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21143) · [TaF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20718)

### Data annotation & validation

**FlipTest, November 15:** nearly identical starting images with different physical outcomes test whether world models track the decisive state. Across 1,680 frames from 10 simulated and 15 real tasks, all 11 tested video models struggle; the best averages 18.3% pair accuracy against 25% random. This challenges visual plausibility as an evaluation proxy, within this benchmark rather than every possible world-model use. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19822)

**Catch-up, November 13: disclose what robot videos show.** A preregistered 9,341-person study across six countries finds that explicit disclosures improve recognition of CGI versus real footage and teleoperation versus autonomy. This is evidence for clearer demonstrations, not a new regulation. [Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18982)

**Catch-up, November 13: NVIDIA's SimReady walkthrough.** OpenUSD profile validation is followed by joint, contact and assembled-scene checks. The example uses known object positions, scripted waypoints and no vision, so passing it does not establish robustness on real hardware. This distinction matters when accepting synthetic training assets. [Technical walkthrough](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-assets-robot-pick-and-place)
