### Data collection

**Catch-up: Eidon closes, with useful assets left behind.** The company's archive says it is no longer operating and makes its glove, seven-IMU tracker, simulation work and datasets public. Its app depended on a backend that has shut down, so “open sourced” does not mean a turnkey operating service. The tracker design is listed at about $280 per kit, a company estimate rather than a current supplier quote. [Company archive](https://www.eidon.ai/)

The paired dataset has 13,451 recordings from 27 contributors, with 24 Hz orientation measurements. Its limitations matter more than the storage headline: laundry accounts for 67.5% of hours, five contributors supply 57.2%, and raw accelerometer/gyro/magnetometer readings appear in only 21.1% of recordings. Orientation data is more widely available. The publisher recommends contributor-based train/test splits and discloses unredacted household video. **Interpretation:** useful collection products need coverage and provenance measures alongside hours. This release does not establish the economics or cause of Eidon’s closure. [Publisher dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/eidon-ai/tracker-pov)

**September 23 · TANDEM makes human time the constrained resource.** It interleaves task-and-motion planning with human teleoperation where the planner lacks a capability, then rechecks the scene before continuing. On a representative task it collected 2.9 times as many demonstrations at matched human intervention time. With 20 demonstrations per task, fine-tuning a pi-0.5-DROID policy raised average success from 0% to 60% across five tasks. This is an under-review result, not a universal multiplier for collection operations. It suggests a deliverable built around difficult stages and recovery, with the autonomous portions recorded too. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28314)

**September 22 · Imperfection for Precision gives lower-grade data a defined role.** The method uses low-precision examples from the target task for high-level context and high-precision examples from other tasks for fine action control. The authors report gains of up to 31.7 percentage points, and an average 4.2-point drop when replacing an equal amount of high-quality task-specific data. The two results answer different comparisons. This supports retaining meaningful quality labels so data can be selected for a recipe, rather than treating every imperfect recording as interchangeable waste. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26672)

**September 23 report · Skyworth XR is bringing head- and wrist-mounted ego capture systems to IROS.** Auganix reports an upcoming showcase, relevant to operators comparing capture hardware. This is a hardware supplier signal, not a collection-buyer request. Product performance and delivery-volume claims were not independently validated in this run. [Report](https://www.auganix.org/robotics-news-skyworth-xr-ego-centric-data-capture-iros-2026/)

### Data annotation & validation

**September 23 · Geometry needs a measurement channel.** “VLMs Can Describe, But Not Measure” evaluates 151 tabletop scenes. It separates object segmentation and semantic annotation from depth-grounded geometry, improving localisation and depth estimates over direct VLM inference. The operational implication is straightforward: a generated spatial description is not a measured pose label. The abstract does not provide a single improvement percentage to quote. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28184)

**September 22 · Audit the retrieval rule separately from the data library.** “Beyond End-Task Success” tests stored experiences across two tasks, three reuse mechanisms and libraries of 3, 10 and 50 examples. A single hindsight-selected experience explains 30–58% of the gap between random selection and an oracle. Its proposed checks include selection-frequency distributions and a best-single-experience baseline. A good success score can otherwise hide a selector that mostly reuses one unusually strong example. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26567)

### Robotics & humanoids

**September 23 · BEE treats human interventions as structured evidence.** It estimates the consistency of corrections along each action dimension, constraining learning more tightly where the human corrections agree. Across three real-world manipulation tasks and one simulation task, the authors report 91.2% average success versus 57.5% for RLT and 42.1% for DSRL at matched online-data budgets. The 91.2% combines real and simulated tasks. It is not fleet reliability. The collection implication is to retain the proposed action, intervention and relevant context rather than only the corrected trajectory. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27450)

### Research papers worth a look

Read **[TANDEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28314)** for selective teleoperation, **[InternW0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27656)** for laboratory capture, and **[BEE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27450)** for intervention data. All three were first submitted September 23.

Also new September 23: **[PointCast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28393)** uses a 19.8M-parameter point-set world-model architecture across rigid objects, cloth, rope and articulated cabinets. It uses **separate checkpoints per regime**, not one universally trained checkpoint. Its planning comparison covers four simulated tasks and 64 episodes, while other evaluations include real teleoperation data. Useful for thinking about persistent point identities as supervision; not evidence of production manipulation across all object types.

For data acceptance and evaluation, retain [Imperfection for Precision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26672) and [the retrieval audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26567), both September 22, plus [depth-grounded VLM scene understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28184), September 23. Results are author-reported and were not reproduced in this run.
