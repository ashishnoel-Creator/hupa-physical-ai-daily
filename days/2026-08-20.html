Binary task success is silently passing up to a quarter of rollouts that damaged the thing they were manipulating. arXiv

    Data collection

RoboEdit-ADC reconstructs and retargets 3D interactions from ordinary RGB human video with no motion capture and no teleoperation rig, producing 174K aligned video pairs across 14M frames and seven embodiments. Read it next to the wider pattern: the automatic-retargeting stack keeps getting better at converting free video into trainable data, so the premium moves to footage that is scarce, specified and provably clean rather than merely plentiful. arXiv

    Data annotation & validation

    • SoftVTBench is the most useful annotation result in weeks because it attacks the label itself. 4,000 expert demonstrations, 50+ assets pairing volumetric deformables with visually matched rigid twins, every episode at 20 Hz synchronizing multi-view RGB, dual-finger tactile RGB and marker motion, proprioception, language and both binary and continuous gripper actions, with FEM state held back as evaluator-only ground truth. The finding is that binary success is not a safe label for deformable work, with 0.7% to 24% of recorded successes violating deformation tolerance in every single in-distribution configuration tested. Second finding worth holding: under distribution shift, visuo-tactile variants won on task success in 6 of 6 comparisons but on deformation-aware success in only 5 of 6, so adding a touch channel does not automatically buy fusion. arXiv
    • Toloka published a named enterprise deployment with Shopify and hard numbers, which is the first primary vendor signal this sweep has surfaced in four consecutive runs. De-identified production conversations are scored by an LLM judge, sub-threshold cases route to Toloka humans for correction, and the corrections feed both SFT golden sets and RL reward signal. Skill-judge accuracy went 75% to 89% in one month from prompt engineering alone with no retraining, and annotation cost roughly halved. Two named work streams: Zero Scores for golden sets, Thumbs Down for merchant-flagged dissatisfaction. That architecture, judge triages and humans fix the tail, is precisely the one robot-rollout validation is converging on. Toloka
    • Toloka also shipped Exams, gated annotator onboarding with three grading modes, configurable inside generation and quorum nodes. Grading runs by exact ground-truth match, by instruction-based LLM-judge comparison for cases like bounding boxes where exact match fails, or against the same criteria used in production. An agent can auto-generate the exam dataset and config. The vendors are starting to compete on provable annotator screening rather than throughput, which is a good sign for anyone selling audit trails. Toloka
    • Dream2Reward (arXiv 2608.18787) learns a language-conditioned reward model from positive demonstrations only, and explicitly needs no failure annotations, no progress labels and no synthetic negatives. It scores an observed latent displacement against a learned successful-transition field using signed directional plus symmetric magnitude agreement, which lets it penalize wrong-direction, overshooting and stagnant motion that progress-based rewards mistakenly score as advancing. The frozen model reduces reward hacking in online and offline policy learning including on real hardware. Note the pricing implication in both directions: it deflates demand for failure labeling and raises the value of verified clean positives. arXiv
    • Prism-GRPO (arXiv 2608.17423) fixes the waste in VLA reinforcement post-training where groups whose rollouts all succeed or all fail carry zero advantage and get thrown away. It splits same-outcome groups using a weighted trajectory-level execution-quality score, with a proof that it never increases discard probability and a guarantee that every success still outranks every failure. Across four RoboTwin tasks it reaches target success with up to 56% fewer rollouts and suppresses a reward-hacking shortcut, and the cleaner behavior transfers to a real robot. The quality signal can come from simulator contacts, executed actions or a VLM, which makes it a labeling spec. arXiv
    • Surge AI launched Tuesday, a Frontier Work Index folding eight of its own benchmarks into one composite score. Chartography, HANDBOOK.md, GDP.pdf, ComplexConstraints, CoreCraft, Hemingway-bench, Antidote and Riemann-bench roll into a single number spanning instruction following, long-context agentic work, professional documents, graphical reasoning, judgment, writing and frontier math. Published leaderboard: Fable 5 at 66.8, GPT-5.6 Sol at 66.7, DeepSeek V4 Pro at 59.7. The strategic read is the interesting one. A data supplier converting its eval portfolio into a branded index it owns and hosts is moving from vendor to standard-setter. Surge AI
    • The Affordance Sheet proposal (arXiv 2608.18317) argues affordance prediction is effectively unbenchmarkable today because of heterogeneous problem formulations, inconsistent dataset annotations, incomplete protocol reporting and missing deployment-condition information. No experiments, no numbers, it is a documentation standard. Worth tracking anyway. A datasheet standard for annotation provenance is the sort of thing worth being early and compliant on rather than retrofitting later. arXiv

    Embedded AI systems

    • Jetson-ORB-SLAM3 (arXiv 2608.17874) is the cleanest edge-deployment result of the window, and its lesson is that the runtime stack is the bottleneck, not the model. The GPU ORB front end on a Jetson Orin Nano reproduces the CPU reference detector to 94.7% exact keypoint agreement and 99.9% descriptor bit agreement, with all four GPU/CPU by Jetson/desktop configurations agreeing within 0.10 cm mean ATE on EuRoC. Generic ONNX-Runtime CUDA and TensorRT providers were unusable for their CosPlace ResNet-50 on device, but a native libnvinfer FP16 engine cut per-query loop-closure inference to 2.2 ms, a 180x speedup, letting learned place recognition run alongside tracking on a 7 W part at 32 FPS mean in monocular-inertial mode. arXiv
    • EcoVLA (arXiv 2608.15502) puts a number on the device-versus-edge question that on-rig inference budgets keep running into: up to 236% better system energy efficiency than existing co-inference approaches under a 20 Hz action-output constraint, while holding SLO satisfaction as network and edge load vary. It builds an architecture-agnostic stage-level abstraction over VLA paradigms, a joint device-edge-network latency and energy model, and a runtime scheme selector with millisecond-level overhead, plus lightweight compression of inter-stage intermediate tensors. From the August 16 mailing and never covered here, so logged now. arXiv
    • Zetta (arXiv 2608.16590) argues that existing agentic harnesses are open-loop, with fixed skills during rollout and reflection only after the episode ends, which cannot govern execution at physical-interaction frequency. It evolves code-based runtime critics and recovery skills online while keeping the base policy frozen, across three timescale-separated loops. Numbers: 90.8% on LIBERO-Pro, 93.6% on RoboCasa, an 11.1x inference speedup, success that keeps scaling with self-exploration experience, and learned skills that transfer zero-shot. Note also that a third independent group has now reached for “harness” as the name for this layer. arXiv
    • NebulaVLA (arXiv 2608.16503) decouples high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action control into an asynchronous dual-frequency architecture, reporting 85.5% average success on LIBERO-Plus and roughly 2.7x faster action generation than synchronous baselines. It adds GESTURE-7, a language-grounded action representation for cross-embodiment transfer, and a Guide Action algorithm enforcing kinematic continuity through mask-based smoothness constraints. Two clock rates in one policy keeps reappearing as the answer to on-device VLA budgets. arXiv
    • Black Sesame Technologies signed a framework chip-supply agreement with Meichuangxi, Midea’s wholly-owned chip platform, starting with industrial and humanoid robots. Black Sesame supplies domestic edge-compute silicon for Meichuangxi’s robotics and home-appliance lines, adaptation and validation first, and is pushing its SesameX embodied platform across Kalos, Aura and Liora modules spanning 48 TOPS to nearly 600 TOPS. Cosmos 3 was presented across the same workflow, from synthetic data generation and neural-simulation policy evaluation through real-time deployment on Jetson Thor.     Robotics & humanoids

    • GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 (arXiv 2608.18234) is the strongest single technical result in the window, and the annotation half of it is the part most people will skip. A causal Transformer jointly predicts next action, next state and the distribution over the next latent behavior command, so implausible commands get retracted onto learned behaviors online. It reports 81.3% success on terrain interaction against the strongest of three large-scale tracker baselines at 4.3x lower, 83.1% under implausible commands, and 99.3% fall recovery at 16.8x the strongest baseline, with the Unitree G1 checkpoint transferring to a Maker L01 on simple fine-tuning. The overlooked part is an automatic terrain-annotation pipeline that recovers full 3D contact geometry from retargeted motion, so terrain labels can be applied across motion datasets that already exist. Value migrating from new capture to relabeling data already on disk is a pattern to watch closely. arXiv
    • Agentic Push-T (arXiv 2608.18227) is four pages of discomfort for demonstration-hungry imitation learning. An LLM coding agent was told to solve Push-T algorithmically with zero demonstration data, found the 2D gym simulator online, ran experiments to learn push mechanics, and reached 100% success using 46% fewer steps than the best diffusion policy trained on 200 human demonstrations. It then self-generated a curriculum extending to Push-A through Push-Z and produced Franka and UR5 cross-embodiment simulation code. The honest response is not to dismiss it. It is to be precise in public about which task classes still need demonstrations, which is contact-rich, unmodelable and open-world work, and Push-T is none of those. arXiv
    • ADEPT (arXiv 2608.19182) pretrains on a generic object-reposing task then post-trains downstream policies with that behavior as prior, using BC distillation, critic warm-up and conservative on-policy updates because naive RL fine-tuning destroys the prior. Teachers distill into perceptive students that zero-shot sim-to-real onto a 23-DoF Kuka-Allegro with two RGB cameras and a 29-DoF Flexiv-Sharpa with two RGB plus five vision-based tactile sensors, solving long-horizon tasks at human-level speed from raw visuo-tactile input. Pre-train then post-train for dexterity, with no human data in the loop. arXiv
    • FetchMan (arXiv 2608.17027) reports that cloning synthetic demonstrations hits a hard performance ceiling regardless of data volume, and that refining the cloned policy with Flow-GRPO on a single sparse reward breaks through it. The sim-to-real pipeline spans more than 150,000 scenes and the reach-and-pick policy deploys zero-shot onto a real Unitree G1 at 73.3% success, walking to and grasping targets in unseen scenes. FetchMan-Bench is released. “Behavior cloning ceilings, reinforcement breaks it” is the transferable claim, and it applies to real demonstrations too. arXiv
    • Xiaomi confirmed its first humanoid on the August 18 earnings call and showed it publicly on August 19, with a nut-tightening success rate improved from 90.2% to 98% across a four-month plant internship. President Lu Weibing put target deployment on Xiaomi’s own smart manufacturing lines, inside the “human, car, home” framing. A real benchmark on a real task from a company with captive factory demand beats another demo reel, and the four-month figure is the honest part. Gizmochina
    • X-Humanoid launched Linglong L2.0, a fire-fighting humanoid with 560 TOPS of onboard compute and over 95% domestically sourced hardware. 1.73 m, 42 active degrees of freedom, stable 3 m/s running, rubble and stair traversal, with visible-light HD cameras plus infrared thermal plus LiDAR plus a multi-parameter gas sensor array for smoke-blind mapping, in a suit rated to 1,000 degrees C. It shuts gas valves and electrical switches autonomously and operates extinguishers by visually locating the fire. Deployed as a fleet alongside a wheeled robot and a quadruped scout. Hazardous-environment work is the deployment wedge where the ROI arithmetic does not depend on wage arbitrage. Gasgoo
    • “The Embodiment Gap in Robot Foundation Models” (arXiv 2608.18433) defines the gap as the work between a reusable model, representation or dataset and actual execution on a target robot, and proposes a reporting framework for adaptation cost that success rate alone hides. It maps existing methods on two axes, type of shared structure and stage at which adaptation is required. Useful as citable vocabulary for why cross-embodiment data has to be sold with its adaptation cost disclosed. arXiv
    • A contrarian result worth flagging (arXiv 2608.15938): action chunking mainly helps short-context policies imitate non-Markovian demonstrations, and with sufficiently long context, closed-loop reactive policies win. That runs against the working assumption in a lot of recent deployment writing, including some of ours. From the August 16 mailing, uncovered until now. arXiv
