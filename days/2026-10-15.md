### Data collection

**October 13 catch-up · capture now extends beyond RGB hours.** EgoExplorer combines professional-task video with reconstructed body/finger motion, tactile observations and step descriptions. EgoWholeView, submitted October 12, instead proposes six synchronized wide-angle RGB cameras for camera-only body and bimanual reconstruction. These represent different capture designs; neither demonstrates that all customers need six cameras or tactile equipment. [EgoExplorer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18872) · [EgoWholeView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16256)

**October 10–13 catch-up · converting human demonstrations into robot data.** DexCast uses RGB-D hand-object reconstruction; Hand2Robot adds object-centric temporal/contact consistency; EgoMatrix generates paired robot observations and actions; EgoCPT optimizes contact from a single ego clip plus reference hand poses. Their input requirements differ. EgoCPT's reported success varies from 67.3% with noisy EgoDex poses to 92.0% with higher-quality TACO reconstructions, illustrating why pose quality and provenance belong in a collection specification. These are author-reported task results, not a universal purchasing standard. [DexCast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14691) · [Hand2Robot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15769) · [EgoMatrix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18822) · [EgoCPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17572)

**October 13 catch-up · richer supervision, not just more demonstrations.** Steertuning uses densely annotated ego pretraining before few-example task adaptation. ForceWAM adds contact play and six-axis force/torque; DexStitch retains skill boundaries and transitions. Interpretation: synchronized signals, clear task outcomes and contact/transition labels are concrete specification topics to discuss with a buyer. They do not prove paid demand for any particular modality. [Steertuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18711) · [ForceWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18852) · [DexStitch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17789)

### Data annotation & validation

**October 12 catch-up · smooth footage is not necessarily successful footage.** A controlled audit of published smoothness-based curation finds strong episode-length confounding and failure cases where useful contact motion is penalized. Its conclusions are limited to the tested datasets and policy configurations. Keep task outcome, intervention and failure-stage labels separate from visual/motion neatness. [Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17427)

**October 13 catch-up · test quality labels against downstream learning.** AXIS-Bench compares weighting, filtering and conditioning using 5,000 naturally suboptimal demonstrations and 24 target tasks with matched budgets. Label granularity interacts with the method/model. RoboFQE offers offline policy evaluation using recorded trajectories and learned terminal-success labels; WorldOracle instead compares predicted rollouts with matched real executions, including failures. These are complementary evaluation approaches, not interchangeable quality certificates. [AXIS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18869) · [RoboFQE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18699) · [WorldOracle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18826)

The paper table also covers sequential trial allocation, adversarial environment generation, runtime risk monitoring and industrial acceptance criteria. Annotation-company searches did not establish a further fresh physical-AI procurement notice beyond the NEURVIX intake above.

### World models & sim-to-real

**October 11–13 catch-up · evaluation needs physically correct failure outcomes.** ProWorld jointly feeds predicted video and robot state back to policies, with failed-interaction training to reduce imagined success. YUBI-Real2Sim evaluates reconstruction artifacts and policy ranking, explicitly stopping short of a general scene-acceptance rule. WorldOracle tests physical state, contact alignment and multi-view coherence against real execution. [ProWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15294) · [YUBI-Real2Sim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17521) · [WorldOracle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18826)

GreenWAM shares an action representation across robot families, while LoopWAM learns from mixed-quality deployment experience without imitating every bad action. Code2Worlds-2 adds executable physics to generated scenes; OccWAM applies occupancy/action coupling to driving. Treat results within each benchmark and embodiment, rather than comparing headline success rates across papers. [GreenWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18774) · [LoopWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18873) · [Code2Worlds-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17651) · [OccWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17688)
