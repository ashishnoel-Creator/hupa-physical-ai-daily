    • Sharpa raised $668M at a $3.3B valuation and put a robot behind the counter of a real Dairy Queen with zero retrofit. September 3, RMB 4.5 billion from Alibaba, Meituan, Tencent, JD.com and Transsion alongside HSG, Qiming, DragonBall and Luminous. On August 29 it opened a DQ outlet on Wujiang Road in Shanghai where the robot runs the full 55-step Blizzard Treat workflow from order to handoff, 10am to 10pm, using the same equipment and ingredients as human staff. Tactile sensing is involved in 98% of those 55 steps. This is the counterexample to the line that no commercial food venue anywhere runs a learned policy, and we should stop using that line without a caveat. KrASIA
    • A former Huawei autonomous-driving AI lead raised $148M to run our exact thesis, and said out loud that data engineering beats model architecture. Morphi Robot, CTO Huang Qingqiu, ex-head of AI for Huawei’s ADS stack. It built its own lightweight wearable capture devices and hired cleaners working in hotels and mixed-use residential and commercial buildings to wear them. The pipeline he describes is filter for bad movement and improperly worn equipment, classify so you can retrieve exactly which data trains which model, auto-label ground truth, evaluate at scale, then go back and mine again where results are weak. His line is that if data quality were infinitely high a very simple model would train to excellent results. Published August 11 and never covered here, which is a real miss. KrASIA

    Data collection

    • Morphi Robot is the closest thing to a funded version of what we are building, and the details are worth reading twice. Covered in the TL;DR above, but three things deserve pulling out. First, the collection workforce is hired service workers in commercial buildings wearing purpose-built lightweight rigs, not teleoperators. Huang says explicitly that the industry is shifting from teleoperation toward portable collection devices because teleop is good for demos in specific scenarios and generalizes badly. Second, his QC framing is measurement not review: identify valid data, then classify it so retrieval is precise. Third, he says a closed-loop data system contains tens of thousands of small details and every one of them is a gap between companies, and he gives centimetre-level relocalization in autonomous driving as the example. That is the operating-discipline argument stated by somebody who has already built the system once at Huawei scale. KrASIA
    • MINT released a 1,021-hour egocentric trajectory dataset and, more usefully, the open labeling pipeline that produced it. arXiv 2609.04958, September 4. It is a single foundation model that goes straight from egocentric RGB to world-space two-hand trajectories, jointly predicting camera trajectory, camera-frame hand states and per-frame hand presence rather than chaining separate stages for camera motion, depth, hand reconstruction and refinement. The honest part: paired world-space camera and hand annotations are scarce, so they built EGOPIPELINE to convert public egocentric video into pseudo-labels, pretrained on those, then fine-tuned on a small set of high-quality joint annotations. Note the abstract ships with literal [xxx] placeholders where the headline improvement numbers should be, so treat the claimed gains as unverified until v2. The released pipeline is the asset regardless. Pseudo-label at volume, then buy a small amount of real annotation quality, is a procurement pattern that changes what a buyer wants from us. arXiv
    • Sharpa’s data story is quieter than its robot and matters more. It collects from first-person human video, simulation and real-world teleoperation together, and argues that commercial settings and homes share enough objects, viewpoints and manipulation patterns for skills to transfer between them. Continuous operation in a live store then generates more real-world data. A company that charges a customer for the deployment and harvests the data off the same shift has a cost structure nobody selling hours can match. KrASIA

    Data annotation & validation

    • ROBORMBENCH shows a VLM reward model will flip the same robot behaviour between failure and success if you reword the instruction. arXiv 2609.05401, September 4, with Jean Oh among the authors, CC BY. 2,390 real-robot trajectories, ground-truth progress labels, 21,673 verified paraphrases across lexical, syntactic and action-goal rewrites. Paraphrasing the instruction alone substantially changes predicted progress scores. The two findings that matter commercially: instability gets worse as rewrites diverge further, and it is not reliably reduced by model scale or by explicit reasoning. What does fix it is dedicated reward models trained with trajectory-grounded supervision. So the fix for a broken grader is aligned annotation, not a bigger model. arXiv
    • FailureSpot names the exact label-noise problem that anyone doing trajectory-level QC is quietly living with. arXiv 2609.04277, September 3. Proactive VLA failure detectors are usually supervised with trajectory-level labels, which means normal pre-failure behaviour inside an unsuccessful trajectory gets labelled as failure. That supervision mismatch injects noise and wrecks both trajectory-level accuracy and timestamp-level localization. Their fix is to build weak supervision out of the unlabelled action chunks themselves, reading abnormal patterns like inconsistent consecutive chunks, frozen or idle actions and aggressive random motions, then use active learning to send only the most uncertain trajectories for expensive timestamp-level annotation. A pass or fail stamp on a whole clip is not an annotation product. Where inside the clip it went wrong is. arXiv
    • LIBERO-RECOVER makes recovery a benchmark, which is the metric we have been arguing is the real unit economics. arXiv 2609.05178, September 4. The setup line is blunt: state-of-the-art hits nearly 100% on LIBERO, which seems to say the models are deployment-ready, and near-perfect scores on existing benchmarks are misleading because success from predefined initial states says nothing about robustness. They collected real execution failures from SOTA embodied models and built 1,000+ scenarios across four recovery levels, action retry, action adaptation, object state recovery and environmental recovery, testing spatial understanding, object structure reasoning, interaction understanding and topological reasoning. The framing shift is from can the robot succeed to can the robot recover. Somebody now has to collect failure-and-recovery data, and that is a different capture spec from clean demonstrations. arXiv, project
    • One Word, Different Action is a real-robot benchmark for the case where identical language means different actions depending on context. arXiv 2609.05260, September 7. Small paper, and it sits directly alongside ROBORMBENCH above. Instruction wording is turning out to be an under-specified part of every capture contract in the field. arXiv
    • What Matters, When? diagnoses conditional visual grounding in visuomotor imitation policies. arXiv 2609.05376, September 7, accepted as an extended abstract at the DexHAND workshop at ECCV 2026. Worth a look because a policy attending to the wrong thing at the wrong moment is a data-coverage problem that no amount of extra hours fixes.

    Embedded AI systems

    • CFAM is the strongest published argument yet for keeping the learning loop on the robot after it ships, and the numbers are specific. arXiv 2609.04552, September 3, ten authors including Zackory Erickson, Giuseppe Loianno, Alexandre Bayen and Jeff Schneider, CC BY. Gradient-free on-device updates after deployment, with a frozen slow-learning component split into Sensor, Reasoning and Action cortices plus a fast Capsule Field that stores field learning one-shot as Competence Capsules. Evaluated across five embodiments, manipulator, quadruped, humanoid, quadrotor and off-road vehicle, against pi0, CogACT and SpatialVLA on the same in-house multi-embodiment dataset. Three numbers to keep: it reaches a standard policy’s operating point on 40% of the data, so 2.5x fewer trajectories; autonomous capture of verified near-OOD cases at test time adds 13.9 percentage points of action success; and backward transfer is -0.5 points against -11.4 for LoRA. The authors are careful that open-world novelty is out of scope. The near-OOD capture result is the one that cuts both ways for us, because a robot that harvests its own hard cases needs fewer bought hours over time. arXiv
    • APEX-RBD is a mixed-precision exploration framework for robot dynamics accelerator design. arXiv 2609.05161, September 7, HKUST, filed under cs.AR rather than cs.RO which is why it does not show up in robotics sweeps. Custom silicon for the dynamics layer specifically, rather than for perception or policy inference, is a thin but growing category worth tracking.
    • Reasoning Without Inference Cost gets chain-of-thought benefits into a VLA policy without paying for them at runtime. arXiv 2609.04893, September 7, from Fei Chen’s group. Latent semantic scaffolding rather than emitted reasoning tokens. Relevant to anyone budgeting inference on a head-worn or edge-bound rig, where the reasoning tax is the thing that kills the power envelope.
    • The FCC robot ban has produced its first real sentiment data and the industry is split exactly down its supply chain. MassRobotics surveyed 14 member companies, from solo founders to organizations of 200+, and published September 7. 43% called the ruling beneficial or very beneficial, 43% called it detrimental or very detrimental, 14% expected no impact. Their read is that sentiment tracks manufacturing footprint almost perfectly. The rule covers ground-based connected robots over 4.4 lb with environmental sensors, at least 200 kbps connectivity and navigation or perception software, and requires 65% domestic component cost to count as US-produced, rising to 75% in 2029. Open responses name motors, sensors, power supplies, DC/DC converters and robot arms as overwhelmingly East Asian, and vetted onshoring supply networks were the single most requested form of support. One respondent said it would have to relocate its headquarters to the US. Waivers run through the Department of War at four to eight weeks. The Robot Report

    Robotics & humanoids

    • Agility’s S-4 is covered in the TL;DR, but the deployment ladder deserves its own line because it is what the valuation is actually pricing. Around 800 Digit v5 units in 2027, 7,000 by 2030, up to 25,000 by 2035. At the illustrative $8,500 monthly RaaS rate that is roughly $2.55 billion of annual subscription revenue at the top of the ladder. To match the revenue from just 1,000 RaaS robots the company needs to grow annual revenue about 57-fold. The alternative model is $200,000 upfront plus a $20,000 deployment fee and about $36,000 a year, roughly $400,000 over five years, so RaaS is worth about $135,000 more per robot to Agility across the same lifespan. Agility itself flags that the unit economics depend on contract terms, timing, pricing and utilization all landing. The Robot Report
    • Sharpa’s DQ deployment breaks a claim we have been repeating, so record it precisely rather than softening it. The three problems Sharpa says it targeted are dependence on customized equipment, difficulty performing complex tasks over long periods, and insufficient reliability for sustained commercial use. Its answer to the first is what it calls zero retrofit, meaning DQ’s equipment, ingredients, procedures and layout are unchanged and the robot uses tools built for human staff. Its answer to the second is decomposition, because training all 55 steps as one trajectory cannot account for every deviation, so it breaks the workflow into subtasks with state evaluation, retry and error recovery after each stage. CraftNet, its hierarchical end-to-end model, takes tactile input and drives the multi-finger Sharpa Wave hand. What is still undisclosed is intervention rate, uptime and units, so this is a strong claim without an audited number behind it. KrASIA
    • AGIBOT took TÜV Rheinland certification and a retail distribution deal at IFA, days after opening the Serbian line. September 4 in Berlin, multi-robot lineup on show, safety certifications from TÜV Rheinland and a partnership with distributor Tekpoint. Manufacturing inside the tariff perimeter plus European safety certification plus a retail channel is a full go-to-market stack, not a trade show appearance. humanoidsdaily
    • LimX and ZINOVA bolted a TRON 2 torso onto heavy industrial arms to assemble formwork and tie rebar. September 4, proof of concept on simulated construction sites, testing ZINOVA’s Tool Intelligence framework in unstructured job sites. A modular humanoid torso on a different lower body is a reminder that the embodiment is becoming a configuration choice, which is exactly the transfer problem the retargeting papers keep attacking. humanoidsdaily
    • TacPAC puts tactile prediction and real-time action correction inside a world-action model. arXiv 2609.05266, September 7, Fudan. Contact-rich manipulation keeps coming back as the bottleneck and keeps getting answered with force and touch rather than more vision. Set it next to Sharpa reporting tactile involvement in 98% of 55 steps and the direction is hard to miss.
    • FWBC-VLA does force-aware whole-body compensation for contact-rich loco-manipulation. arXiv 2609.03889, September 4. Same theme, humanoid scale.
    • Two smaller items. Tuya unveiled Doova at IFA on September 5, a mobile companion robot for elder care combining fall detection, multimodal LLM conversation and AIoT control (humanoidsdaily). SocioGesture, arXiv 2609.04545, does real-time adaptive social gesture perception with Chip Huyen and Jan Liphardt among the authors, and non-verbal human behaviour remains a capture category almost nobody is collecting for (arXiv).

    Twitter / X signal

    • Fourth consecutive run with no X coverage, but the failure is now understood and it is two separate failures, not one. All five Chrome handshake retry cycles were run per protocol. Cycles one and two were plain tabs_context_mcp retries, cycle three re-ran select_browser first, cycle four attempted tabs_create_mcp, cycle five went back to list_connected_browsers. list_connected_browsers returned a live local device every single time and select_browser succeeded every single time. Every tabs_context_mcp call timed out. So the extension connects and the tab-group handshake never completes, consistently, which makes this a reproducible defect rather than flakiness.

    Research papers worth a look

    Papers already written up above are not repeated here. What follows is the rest of the September 4 to 7 batch.

    • RoboSPA: Can VLA Models Go Beyond Simple Scenes and Short-Horizon Tasks? arXiv 2609.05324, September 7, Zhejiang University, accepted at EMNLP 2026 main conference. The title is the finding. Worth reading next to LIBERO-RECOVER above, because both are attacks on the same problem, that current benchmarks are too easy to tell you anything about deployment. arXiv
    • VLA-Precision: Asymmetric Co-Bootstrapping for Efficient Real-World Online RL of Vision-Language-Action Models. arXiv 2609.04355, September 4, USTC, 17 pages. Real-world online RL rather than sim, which is the expensive kind. Anything that lowers the cost of the correction loop pushes value toward on-policy correction data and away from bulk demonstrations. arXiv
    • GIFT: Guided Intermediate Feature Training via Action-Oriented Structural Supervision for Robotic Manipulation. arXiv 2609.04193, September 4, CASIA and Tsinghua with Shuicheng Yan and Ya-Qin Zhang among fourteen authors. Supervising intermediate features with action-oriented structure rather than only the final action. Structural supervision is an annotation product nobody currently sells. arXiv
    • Toward Unified Robot Learning: Bridging Representation, Vision-Language-Action, and World Models. arXiv 2609.03927, September 4, published in TMLR 2026. A survey, and a useful one right now because the VLA and world-model camps have been talking past each other all year. Good orientation document if anyone needs to come up to speed fast. arXiv
    • Two models trending on Hugging Face that have not appeared in any prior brief. PerceptronAI’s Isaac-0.5, a 36B robotics model updated around September 2, currently second on the robotics trending list at 341 downloads and 33 likes (HF). SIMFORGE-AI’s SIMFORGE-D1.5, 7B, updated around September 1 (HF). Both are small numbers today and both are worth a standing watch, because the robotics trending list has been a reliable two-week leading indicator on which open stacks buyers start asking about.
    • Coverage note carried forward from yesterday, because it is still true and nothing this window changed it. No paper in this batch treats annotation quality methodology for robot data as its own subject. ROBORMBENCH and FailureSpot both get close and both frame it as a model problem rather than a process problem. The category has benchmarks, verifiers, datasets and now a recovery benchmark, and still no methodology literature.
