    Data collection

    • The Moqi Intelligence playbook is now on record, and it reads like an ego-data operating manual. Huang Qingqiu, ex-Huawei “Genius Youth” and former head of Huawei’s autonomous driving AI division, told 36Kr how his startup (1B+ yuan in angel financing) runs collection: self-developed lightweight wearable devices, cleaners hired to wear them during real work in hotels and commercial-residential apartments, then strict quality screening to filter valid data, categorization for retrieval, and automatic annotation to generate training ground truth. His thesis is blunt: “assuming the data quality is infinitely high, even the simplest model can be trained to produce excellent results,” and the industry is moving from teleoperation to portable collection. He also says the gap between companies lives in thousands of small details of the data closed loop, like centimeter-level re-localization that most companies cannot achieve. Wearable capture during paid work plus validated QC is exactly the shape of pipeline we argue for, and a Huawei-pedigree team just raised a billion yuan to run it in China. 36Kr
    • Ego-OSCAR is the collection story on the research side. Sub-$200 BOM from commercial components and 3D-printed parts, stereo plus synchronized IMU, a full open software stack, and ~550 hours of in-the-wild indoor ego video already collected through a distributed contributor network. The economics matter more than the specs: if a defensible crowdsourced rig costs $200, the premium shifts entirely to what the hardware alone cannot give you, which is validated capture against a spec, demographic and environment distribution, and an audit trail. arXiv
    • Sekai2 (arXiv 2608.09449, Shanghai AI Lab lineage) released 128,892 clips totaling 2,826 hours of real-world exploration video from 10,428 source videos across 113 countries, weighted toward sustained observation for interactive world modeling. World-model training corpora keep getting assembled from found and crowdsourced video at thousands-of-hours scale. arXiv
    • A CMU team built a high-fidelity capture framework for robot-assisted bathing and recorded clinicians performing bathing demonstrations on human subjects, claiming the first dataset with synchronized motion, shape, contact and force during sustained contact-rich human-human interaction (arXiv 2608.09127). Contact-rich care work is about the hardest capture spec there is, and someone just published the rig design. All materials to be released. arXiv
    • PhyS-120K (arXiv 2608.07981, Shanghai AI Lab authors) is a 120K-video dataset of real-world physical interactions spanning rigid-body dynamics, soft-body deformation, fluids and phase transitions, each annotated with structured physical labels, used to distill physical priors into streaming world models. Physics-annotated real video as a product category. arXiv
    • TAMS (arXiv 2608.09731) does task-aware multi-view adaptive streaming for wireless teleoperation: under the most constrained bandwidth it cut completion time from 68.9s to 43.9s and lifted trial success from 48% to 71% by prioritizing the camera view the operator actually needs. Teleop rig bandwidth engineering translating directly into demonstrations per paid hour. arXiv

    Data annotation & validation

    • RynnValue (arXiv 2608.09853, Alibaba’s Rynn robotics line) scales a value foundation model to 7,000+ hours and ~3M instruction-conditioned clips using temporal distance as supervision, with no preference or progress annotations at all, and beats the fully preference-supervised state of the art (Kendall’s tau 0.675 vs 0.655). Converted into dense rewards it lifts real-world policy success from 52.5% to 72.5% online. The uncomfortable read for annotation vendors: a major lab just showed timestamps can replace a whole class of human labels. The counter-read: the clips still need instruction conditioning and curation, which is upstream labeling by another name. arXiv
    • MMPF (arXiv 2608.07895, University of Tokyo Matsuo-lab cluster with Mitsubishi Electric co-authorship) audits robot demonstration datasets for instruction-trajectory mismatches: behaviorally correct trajectories paired with the wrong language instruction. Training-free detection and label correction, with downstream policy gains when the audit runs before training. Demo-data auditing as its own pipeline stage is precisely the validated-data argument, published. arXiv
    • ALVA (arXiv 2608.08273, KAIST) is a trajectory evaluator conditioned on observations, executed actions and the language instruction, with near-zero false-positive rates in 3D household sim, usable as terminal feedback for closed-loop policy optimization. The rollout-scoring literature keeps compounding, and every one of these evaluators is trained on judged rollouts. arXiv
    • WorldSimProbe (arXiv 2608.09298) formalizes an “Observable Simulator Contract” for action-conditioned world models and evaluates six open ACWMs on 18,000+ instances, finding systematic action-realization degradation and structured failures in interaction grounding. World models are now getting the QC treatment that used to be reserved for policies. Pairs with GAUGE from last week. arXiv
    • Surge AI published the only on-topic-adjacent vendor result of the window: post-training a model on non-coding office work added +5.8 points on SWE-Bench Pro, which Surge attributes to transferable goal-directed execution. Cross-domain transfer changes what data vendors can charge for out-of-domain corpora. Surge
    • The other named vendors (Scale, Mercor, Labelbox, Snorkel, Sama, iMerit, Invisible, Centific, Toloka) published nothing on-thesis dated this window.

    Embedded AI systems

    • SLIM-0.5B (arXiv 2608.09771) is a compact 0.5B-parameter latent interaction policy that matches or exceeds large VLA and world-action-model baselines with lower inference latency and substantially lower GPU memory, and no embodied pretraining. The small-model counterattack on VLA compute budgets continues. arXiv
    • WA-SpecDec (arXiv 2608.08725) injects world-model-derived scene awareness into VLA speculative decoding: 1.5x matched-success speedup over speculative decoding alone and 18.6% fewer near-contact failures. Inference acceleration and safety improving together rather than trading off. arXiv
    • SpeedTuning (arXiv 2608.09138, Stanford, with Tony Zhao and Chelsea Finn on the author list) learns to predict optimal execution speed for a base policy’s actions with lightweight RL, exceeding 2.4x speedup on pouring, throwing and precise picking without new data collection. Cycle time is unit economics, and this attacks it at the policy layer. arXiv
    • Tencent quietly pushed Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA to Hugging Face around August 8, in UMI and RoboTwin variants at 5B parameters. Hunyuan’s embodied line going open-weights is a second Chinese big-tech VLA release channel after Alibaba’s Rynn family. Hugging Face
    • VicOne released a free cybersecurity testing extension for NVIDIA Isaac Sim, built from DEF CON 34 research, so developers can fuzz robot cyber safety before deployment. Security validation entering the standard sim toolchain, a week after the physical prompt-injection paper showed why. The Robot Report
