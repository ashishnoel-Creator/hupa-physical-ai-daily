    Data collection

    • The Physen AI detail is the collection story of the window. Its lineup is exactly the rig stack: head-mounted EGO terminals, data gloves and wristbands built on inertial sensing, synchronously capturing visuals, motion trajectories and force feedback. W-Ibeda, a Chinese high-precision IMU maker, visited Physen in Shenzhen and the two agreed to jointly target the North American market for robot data acquisition equipment. The buried lede is Physen’s customer list: a supply partnership with Figure, plus integrations in progress with Amazon and DoorDash. Gasgoo calls it one of the few Chinese firms to commercialize capture hardware overseas. The capture-hardware layer is globalizing from China outward, and the US labs are buying. Gasgoo
    • INFIFORCE’s funding rationale reads like our own thesis doc: third-person demonstration data produces robots that mimic but cannot interpret their environment first-person, so since 2025 the company has run an “Ego-Centric data strategy” capturing first-person interaction from actual human operations. Its argument for the approach: dramatically lower cost per high-quality hour than physical robot collection, plus richer real-world bandwidth. Nearly 1B yuan of Series A/A+ money from Dunhong Asset, state-owned platforms, Zhejiang University’s sci-tech group and CCV is now behind it. Full read in China spotlight. Gasgoo
    • DexTeleop signed a strategic partnership with JD.com on August 12 and framed the JD Seven Fresh supermarket deployments explicitly as data plays: TA-series robots restocking shelves and delivering samples in Taiyuan now, Beijing and Tianjin next, “to gather more real-world data.” JD opens its RoboBase industrial infrastructure and its user data for joint consumer products, and DexTeleop becomes preferred partner for JD’s robotics procurement. Retail floors as capture environments, with the retailer as distribution partner. Gasgoo
    • HandEdit (arXiv 2608.12122, SJTU/Fudan cluster with Hongyang Li and Li Chen on the author list) is a 200M-instance dataset and benchmark for editing human hands and arms into 26 different robot embodiments inside egocentric frames. The premise stated flat in the abstract: embodiment-aware teleop data is too expensive, egocentric human video is the scalable alternative, and the gap between them is now its own supervised learning problem. Every paper like this raises the value of well-shot ego hours. arXiv
    • EgoPHI (arXiv 2608.13014, ETH Zurich, Christian Holz’s lab, ECCV 2026) jointly estimates dense contact maps and 3D force distributions on hand and object meshes from a single monocular egocentric RGB frame. Ground-truth force labels don’t scale, so they built a physics-simulation pipeline to add per-vertex force supervision to existing hand-object datasets, then validated on instrumented physical objects with eight participants. Force ground truth from plain ego video is the kind of derived signal that makes raw capture hours worth more after the fact. arXiv
    • NestDex (arXiv 2608.13362) wraps dexterous teleoperation in a copilot: a nested policy assists the human operator during collection itself. Assisted teleop means more usable demonstrations per paid hour, which is rig economics. arXiv

    Data annotation & validation

    • Decoding Task Progress from VLA Representations (arXiv 2608.13474, Cornell-affiliated authors with Wei-Chiu Ma and Preston Culbertson) probes whether a policy’s own internal representations encode how far through a task it is. If task progress is linearly readable from the model, that is a free progress label on every trajectory, and a building block for automated QC of demonstration completeness. arXiv
    • ContactGuard (arXiv 2608.13438, CMU/Vanderbilt lineage) does pre-contact execution monitoring with action-conditioned latent world models, flagging failures before the robot touches anything. The runtime-monitoring literature keeps converging on the same shape: a validation layer between policy and world, which is QC productized as middleware. arXiv
    • Attune (arXiv 2608.12650, UIST 2026) is a self-annotation tool for robot operator attention profiles: operators label their own attention traces during supervision sessions. Small paper, but the workflow, humans annotating their own operating streams, is a labor pattern to watch for teleop fleets. arXiv

    Robotics & humanoids

    • The Unitree numbers deserve unpacking. Roughly 18,000 cumulative bipedal humanoids produced, G1 alone past 11,000 by June against 6,500 for all of 2025. SAG’s market frame: 19,100 global humanoid shipments in H1 2026, Chinese manufacturers above 97%, Unitree at 5,900 units and 31% share. The IPO proceeds (6.1B yuan) fund a manufacturing base designed for 190,000 units a year, 75,000 humanoid and 115,000 quadruped, on a two-year build. Whoever wins the humanoid brain, the bodies are being made in Hangzhou. Gasgoo
    • G0.5 (arXiv 2608.11739, Hang Zhao’s Tsinghua/Galaxea line, 27 authors) folds reasoning and action into one autoregressive stream, one transformer decoder, one objective, and posts big numbers: 76.7% real-world fine-tuned success vs 53.3% for pi0.5 and 24.4% for GR00T-N1.7, plus wins on BEHAVIOR, DROID zero-shot transfer (82.5%), LIBERO (98.9%) and RoboTwin 2.0 (93.3%). The architectural claim is that the VLM should be the decision-maker, not a context encoder for a bolted-on action expert. If the single-stream recipe holds up, pretraining data mix (robot datasets plus VQA) becomes even more of the differentiator. arXiv
    • DreamX-Phi 1.0 dropped as an open action-conditioned video world model for manipulation from Alibaba’s AMAP mapping division, with code on GitHub. The map-and-navigation unit of Alibaba shipping robot world models says something about how broadly the WAM race has spread inside Chinese big tech. arXiv
    • CasiaHand Robotics, the CASIA dexterous-hand spinout, partnered with Fulai New Materials to scale production of dexterous robotic hands. Hands are the bottleneck component everyone names; a materials supplier signing on for volume is a supply-chain maturity marker. Gasgoo
    • Deliberate Practice (arXiv 2608.13415, Brown, Konidaris group) computes provably budget-optimal allocations of limited robot practice time across skills, solving which skills to practice as a bilinear program. Data acquisition under an explicit budget is now a formal optimization problem in the literature. The same math applies to deciding which demonstration hours to buy. arXiv
