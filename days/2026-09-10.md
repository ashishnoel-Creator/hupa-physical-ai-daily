### Data collection

- **Figure's Index numbers are in the TL;DR and they are the collection story of the window.** One extra thing worth pulling out separately. The $15M paid to contributors against 16M uploads works out to under a dollar per upload. That is the going rate a well-capitalised buyer is willing to pay an untrained global crowd for unvalidated ego video, and anyone pricing validated capture should know that number sits underneath them. [Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/figure-ai-unveils-index-crowdsourcing-real-world-human-video-to-train-helix)
- **Coherent4D is a 233K-sample egocentric dataset that pairs future interaction location with the body motion to get there.** Released September 8 with the paper "From Where to How: Continuous 4D Interaction Forecasting from Egocentric Video." Three domains. The interesting design choice is that where and how are labelled jointly rather than as two separate annotation passes, which is exactly the thing that makes an ego corpus expensive to relabel later. [arXiv 2609.08636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08636)
- **DYAD is small on purpose and says so.** 20 sessions of gearbox assembly with a HoloLens 2 wearer and one trained helper, linking 528 task-step intervals to 611 requests for help and 851 assistance records. The paper explicitly frames its contribution as linked interaction structure rather than scale. On 829 mode events the best RGB-only model gets 0.548 macro-F1, causal metadata takes it to 0.624, and a privileged trigger mapping gets 0.915. Thats a 37-point gap between what pixels give you and what the right annotation gives you on identical footage. [arXiv 2609.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09023)
- **Kairos went after the thing nobody wants to pay for, which is long video with dense time-resolved labels.** Ten-minute to half-hour clips annotated for actions, entities, attributes and interactions at fine temporal granularity. Most video corpora are long and sparsely aligned or short and densely aligned. This one refuses the trade. [arXiv 2609.08755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08755)
- **Pollen Robotics shipped a small emotion-animation dataset for its Microduck platform on September 7.** Audio, video and tabular, Apache-2.0, 198 downloads already. Tiny, and logged only because Pollen keeps quietly putting real capture out under a permissive licence while larger players gate theirs. [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pollen-robotics/microduck-emotions)

### Data annotation & validation

- **EXL has completed its $310M acquisition of iMerit, and this brief missed it, so here it is properly.** $170M upfront plus $140M in earnouts over two years. Radha Basu joins EXL as EVP and head of iMerit. iMerit does annotation for robotics, autonomous mobility and healthcare AI, and brings the Ango Hub platform plus a global expert network into a 68,000-person BPO. Two things matter. First, an Indian-founded annotation business just cleared $310M, which is a real comparable where before there was mostly speculation. Second, the buyer is a business-process company, not a lab and not a data-labelling peer. Basu's framing in the deal interview is the tell: "the final mile and the concept of trust need work." [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/exl-acquires-physical-ai-model-developer-imerit/), [EXL newsroom](https://www.exlservice.com/about/newsroom/exl-to-acquire-imerit-advancing-its-leadership-in-enterprise-ai-by-adding-foundation-model-expertise-and-technology)
- **CALIPER is the most useful thing published this window for anyone writing a capture spec.** It tests whether a pretrained visual representation can actually infer physics, by striking an object twice at known speeds and then asking a linear probe to predict how far a third strike slides it. In a clean fixed-camera scene every representation tested, all the way down to a randomly initialised ViT and raw pixels, lands within 0.02 R² of the true-simulator ceiling. Resample the camera, lighting and clutter and the same set spreads across 0.50 R². Erasing the probed mass direction costs nothing in the clean scene and 0.35 R² in the varied one. Read plainly: a clean, controlled, well-lit collection environment cannot tell you whether your data is teaching physics or teaching wallpaper. Variation is not a nice-to-have in the spec, it is the only condition under which the benchmark discriminates at all. [arXiv 2609.08250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08250)
- **RoboReel is a benchmark for learning-from-observation policies, which is the category that eats human video.** Ten manipulation tasks, four test suites covering visual distractors and long-horizon completion, and it bundles the human demonstration videos, the sim robot trajectories and the eval environments together instead of leaving each group to assemble its own. Seven-plus LfO algorithms evaluated. The stated finding is that long-horizon and low-tolerance tasks are still hard. If human-video-to-policy is the pitch, this is now the scoreboard. [arXiv 2609.08209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08209)
- **Surge published a result that argues against task-matched data collection.** They post-trained on non-coding office work and got +5.8 points on SWE-Bench Pro, and attribute it to transferable goal-directed execution rather than to anything code-specific. Take it with a pinch of salt given who is selling it. But if it holds, the "collect exactly the task you want to deploy" instinct is leaving value on the table, and generic competent human work has more transfer than the market prices. [Surge](https://www.surgehq.ai/blog/office-work-post-training-improves-coding)
- **Mercor shipped APEX-Agents 1.1 on September 8.** A leaderboard update, not a research release. Logged because Mercor keeps moving toward owning the evaluation surface rather than the labelling surface, and that is the more defensible half. [Mercor](https://www.mercor.com/blog/introducing-apex-agents-1-1/)
- **iMerit also published a working note on evaluation awareness, which is a real problem and nobody in robotics data is talking about it yet.** The claim is that models behave differently when they can tell they are being tested. For a policy evaluated on a held-out set collected the same way as its training set, that failure mode is very very hard to see. [iMerit](https://imerit.net/blog/)

### Embedded AI systems

- **ComVLA cuts a VLA's visual token budget from 512 to 32 and loses 1.5 points of task success.** 74% less inference compute, 22% less latency, 95.4% vs 96.9% on LIBERO, holding under Rayleigh and Rician fading. The mechanism is language guidance deciding which visual tokens matter, so the budget adapts to channel capacity. This is the split-inference case being made properly for once, with the cost stated instead of buried. [arXiv 2609.07838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07838)
- **A ROS 2 vulnerability lets an attacker hijack a robot and fake the telemetry that would have caught it.** One environment variable is enough to load a pre-built hook that intercepts and injects control signals and telemetry before publication, so the remote attestation layer sees synthesized data and reports healthy. Demonstrated on a physical Franka Emika arm running Secure ROS 2, with roughly 3ms of jitter and an 87% success rate against an AI-based detector. It scales through third-party Docker containers. Responsibly disclosed. Anyone building an audit trail on top of robot telemetry should read this, because the whole point of an audit trail is that it cannot be the thing being spoofed. [arXiv 2609.08280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08280)
- **Xiaomi open-sourced the U0 weights on September 8 under Apache-2.0.** Three repos, a 34B autoregressive world foundation model for embodied synthesis initialized from EMU3.5, plus the FSDP training code. Scene generation, transfer, and interleaved subtask and video rollout. Four likes at time of checking, which says nothing yet. A 34B world model from a consumer-electronics giant under a real permissive licence is worth watching regardless of the like count. [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/XiaomiRobotics/Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-4B)
- **ByteDance is reportedly building a real-time spatial video model, with Zhang Yiming personally involved.** Reported September 8. No paper, no weights, no benchmark, so treat it as a rumour with a good source. Filed because the last three of these turned into products. [TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/09/08/bytedance-real-time-spatial-video-model-zhang-yiming/)

### Robotics & humanoids

- **XPeng's production line is in the TL;DR.** One clause of context, because it changes what the number means: XPeng disclosed IRON's 2,250 TOPS across three in-house Turing chips back in August, so this is the same robot moving from spec sheet to line, not a new machine.
- **TANGO puts a whole-body VLA on a Unitree G1 zero-shot with no real navigation data.** Natural-language instruction plus egocentric RGB in, 29-DoF joint-space actions out. Trained entirely in simulation through synthesized collision-free traversal. No numbers in the abstract, which is a caveat, but a language-conditioned humanoid walking through clutter on sim-only training is a real milestone for the sim-first camp. [arXiv 2609.09158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09158)
- **Dex-X gets 93% on real-world cube picking from human video, using simulation as a tactile completion engine.** The trick is neat. Monocular human video has no tactile channel, so they reconstruct the hand-object interaction in sim, let contact dynamics generate the tactile supervision the video never had, then distill into a point-cloud plus tactile policy. Zero robot-side data collection at all. Teacher hits 65.9% across six sim task categories, distilled policy hits 93% on cube picking and 53% on table cleaning. The gap between those two real numbers is the honest part. [arXiv 2609.07747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07747)
- **DeCAL reports 71% average success and 83.4% progress success on dexterous tasks.** Mixture-of-Transformers with separate experts for understanding, imagination and action, plus contact-aware gating that decides how much to trust the tactile channel. Same week as Dex-X and TacPAC, so tactile-inside-the-policy is now three independent groups deep. [arXiv 2609.09119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09119)
- **Vention opened a Physical AI Lab in Montreal on September 9, aimed at getting manipulation from research to production lines.** A manufacturing-automation company building its own physical AI capability in-house rather than buying it is the pattern that should worry anyone selling that capability as a service. [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/vention-opens-physical-ai-lab-manufacturing-montreal/)
- **Two Boston Dynamics veterans launched Dynamic Creatures on September 8 to build robot characters.** Marc Theermann as CEO and Farbod Farshidian as CTO. Own line of characters plus recreations for brands. Entertainment robotics is a different business from labour robotics and it monetizes years earlier. [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/boston-dynamics-veterans-launch-dynamic-creatures-to-bring-characters-to-life-with-robotics/)
- **FolDeX is a real-robot-only benchmark for long-horizon deformable manipulation, built around garment folding.** The design principle is the interesting bit. It is organized around reusing heterogeneous physical experience, including the human intervention and recovery data generated during deployment, on the explicit premise that fresh real-robot collection is too expensive. That is the correction-loop economics argument showing up inside a benchmark. [arXiv 2609.10243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10243)
- **Medtronic put $700M into Cornerstone Robotics.** Surgical, so off our four wedges, but it is the largest single strategic robotics cheque in the window and worth knowing the number. [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/medtronic-invests-700m-in-surgical-partner-cornerstone-robotics/)

- **Brett Adcock, September 9, is the highest-value post of the window and is in the TL;DR.** One line from it not in the TL;DR: he framed the current phase as "scaling intelligence through data and compute" and explicitly contrasted it with the parts of the humanoid race money cannot buy. Figure is saying out loud that it has moved past the hardware chapter. [post](https://x.com/adcock_brett/status/2097001024397651994)
- **Chris Paxton had the most substantive researcher thread of the window, on reward specification.** His argument is that on-robot RL needs reward functions, ideal rewards give a rich improvement signal, and the only two options that actually scale are binary success-or-failure annotations or binary human preference. Both are information-poor. That is a labelling problem dressed as an RL problem, and it is the same trade the annotation vendors are circling from the other side. [post](https://x.com/chris_j_paxton/status/2097677205581050258)
- **Paxton also flagged LightOrigins training a generalist policy across hardware impairments,** so a robot adapts instead of halting when a component degrades. His read is that redundancy is the underrated argument for the humanoid form factor. Failure-mode data as a training input rather than a discard is the same idea FolDeX is built on. [post](https://x.com/chris_j_paxton/status/2097710936987431255)
- **Deepak Pathak demonstrated physical prompt steerability on S1 and Paxton called it a prerequisite rather than a result.** Four different prompts producing four distinct behaviours in the same environment. Correct framing. Steerability is the floor for language-conditioned policies, not the ceiling. [post](https://x.com/chris_j_paxton/status/2097736088076886478)

### Research papers worth a look

- **CALIPER: Clean Scenes Cannot Rank Physical Inference in Pretrained Visual Representations.** Covered under annotation above. The single most quotable finding of the window for anyone who writes capture specs for a living. [arXiv 2609.08250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08250)
- **Monkey See, Can Monkey Do? A Benchmark for Evaluating Robot Skill Learning by Observation.** Introduces RoboReel. CoRL 2026, 31 pages. [arXiv 2609.08209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08209)
- **DYAD: A Multimodal Dataset of Co-Located Human Assistance.** The 0.548 to 0.915 spread on identical footage is the number. [arXiv 2609.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09023)
- **From Where to How: Continuous 4D Interaction Forecasting from Egocentric Video.** Coherent4D, 233K samples, three domains. [arXiv 2609.08636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08636)
- **Kairos: A Dataset for Fine-Grained Video-Language Modeling over Space, Time, and Dynamics.** Ten-minute to half-hour videos with dense temporal annotation. [arXiv 2609.08755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08755)
- **Dex-X: Learning Visual-Tactile Dexterous Manipulation From Human Videos with Simulated Interaction.** 93% real cube picking, zero robot-side collection. [arXiv 2609.07747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07747)
- **DeCAL: Towards Physically-Grounded Dexterous Vision-Language-Action Models via Contact-Aware Latent Co-Imagination.** 71% average, 83.4% progress success. [arXiv 2609.09119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09119)
- **TANGO: Humanoid Navigation in Cluttered Environments with a Whole-Body Vision-Language-Action Model.** 29-DoF, sim-only training, zero-shot on a Unitree G1. [arXiv 2609.09158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09158)
- **ComVLA: Communication-Aware Split Inference for VLA Models in 6G-Connected Robotics.** 512 tokens down to 32 for 1.5 points of success. [arXiv 2609.07838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07838)
- **Seeing is Not Believing: Breaking the Physical-to-Digital Trust Boundary in Robotics.** ROS 2 telemetry spoofing on a live Franka arm, 87% evasion. [arXiv 2609.08280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08280)
- **FolDeX: A Physical-World Benchmark for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation of Deformable Objects.** Built around reusing deployment-time intervention and recovery data. [arXiv 2609.10243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10243)
- **Xiaomi-Robotics-U0.** 34B embodied world foundation model, Apache-2.0, weights and FSDP training code. [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/XiaomiRobotics/Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-4B)
