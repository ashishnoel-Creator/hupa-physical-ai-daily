### Data collection

- **Dyna-2 is the item of the window and it lands directly on the wedge.** More than 1M hours of egocentric human video in pretraining, roughly 170 years of waking experience, with a claimed first human-to-robot scaling law running from 1k to 1M hours. The part that matters commercially is the transfer claim: the scaling curve holds on zero-shot offline evaluation against robot data the model never saw. If that survives replication, hours of clean human ego video become a priced input with a published elasticity, which is the single most useful thing that could happen to a capture business. Treat the numbers as provisional given the issued correction. [Dyna Robotics](https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2)
- **Ropedia in Singapore is building a wearable capture device called HOMIE on $30M total funding, $22M of it a pre-Series A, and its co-founder gave an interview on August 20 that could have been lifted from our own framing.** The stated thesis is that hardware sets what a robot can physically do and data sets how many useful things it knows, and that the bottleneck is shifting to the latter. Roadmap is more sensing modalities and higher-quality annotation, plus an argument that Singapore should be a living testbed across logistics, construction and hospitality. This is the closest comparable to surface in months and it is funded, regional and pointed at the same buyers. [TechNode](https://technode.global/2026/08/20/robots-need-real-world-data-not-just-better-hardware-says-ropedia-co-founder-qa/)
- **Axis Robotics raised a $12M seed led by Hack VC for a browser-based remote collection plus mobile capture stack, claiming 100,000+ global contributors and 20,000+ hours of real-world data plus 1,200+ hours of sim data per month.** Google News resurfaced it on August 23 but the original date is about July 27, so it is a back-fill rather than fresh. Contributor-network capture at that claimed throughput is a different shape of competitor from a rig company, and the per-month figure is the one to check rather than the contributor count. [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/07/27/axis-robotics-emerges-from-stealth-with-12m-to-build-the-data-engine-for-physical-ai/)
- **DreamHand (arXiv 2608.20308) repurposes a video diffusion model as a deterministic geometry encoder and cuts hand-pose error by 30% on ARCTIC and 40% on HOT3D, with gains of 46% to 61% once out-of-sight hands are counted.** A ray-based camera solver variant needs no test-time camera intrinsics and it runs with no external detector. This attacks the exact failure that makes raw headcam footage unusable, which is hands leaving frame, and dropping the intrinsics requirement means uncalibrated consumer-grade capture becomes viable. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20308)
- **Koala Gripper (arXiv 2608.20546) from the Robotics and AI Institute argues that handheld capture devices copy existing gripper morphologies and sacrifice ergonomics and manipulation performance, so the capture device and the robot gripper should be co-designed.** The only quantified claim in the abstract is backdrivable actuated fingers with effective mass on the order of tens of grams, so treat it as a position paper with hardware attached. It is the capture-device-as-first-class-design-problem argument made by an institute rather than by a vendor, which makes it citable in investor material. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20546)
- **EgoHRV (arXiv 2608.18711) pulls blood volume pulse from the gaze cameras already inside egocentric headsets, no added sensor, and feeding the resulting heart-rate-variability estimates into EgoExo4D's proficiency estimator raises accuracy by 17.8%.** Code is released. A free physiological label channel derived from hardware already on the head is a data-product differentiator, and proficiency scoring is exactly the kind of derived label buyers pay for. It is also a privacy question worth answering before somebody asks it. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18711)
- **What Matters for Latent Actions in Robot Learning (arXiv 2608.19613) is the first comprehensive study of Latent Action Models, testing 41 design choices and 4 proxy metrics under one autoencoding framework, and concludes that fine-tuning VLM backbones with latent actions gives a stronger initialization for downstream policy learning.** LAMs are the mechanism that turns unlabeled human video into robot training signal, so this is the reference on which design choices actually pay off. It should shape how raw ego video gets packaged before it is sold. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19613)
- **Grounded-Exo2Ego (arXiv 2608.20534) names camera-reconstruction misalignment as a previously overlooked failure in exocentric-to-egocentric video generation and ships a fully automated synthetic data engine using rigged 3D characters in procedural environments.** The abstract claims large margins over state of the art across all metrics on EgoExo4D without giving numbers, so discount accordingly. Track it as a substitute threat, with the caveat that the authors concede the task is significantly harder than novel view synthesis. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20534)
- **A VLM survey on egocentric video (arXiv 2608.18671) reports that current models recognize visible objects far more reliably than they recognize evolving interactions, actions and user intent, especially over long activities.** No experiments, it is a survey. The value is a citable third-party statement that the bottleneck in ego video is interaction-aware supervision rather than more raw pixels, which is the annotation argument stated by somebody with no commercial stake in it. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18671)

### Data annotation & validation

- **Rethinking Demonstration Unlearning (arXiv 2608.20784) is the first serious treatment of what happens when a contributor asks for their demonstrations to be deleted, and the finding is that the standard metrics do not hold together.** It audits on two axes, behavior and evidence, across five preregistered conditions, three real-robot policy classes and two simulation suites, and shows the axes dissociate in both directions: an edit can repair behavior while leaving membership evidence intact, or scrub evidence while moving behavior away from a true retrain. On the ACT arm a redirect edit restored blind-scored success to 18 of 20 trials. Anyone selling human-collected ego data will meet this contractually, and the paper shows the off-the-shelf answer does not work. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20784)
- **Beyond Imitation (arXiv 2608.21204) shows failed rollouts carry trainable value that behavior cloning discards, with real bimanual stack-cups going 40% to 90% and insert-wallet 25% to 80% in five iterations with frozen BC weights and no human intervention.** Supervised fine-tuning on successful rollouts alone stalls at 55% and 30% on the same tasks. Simulation: LIBERO-10 93% to 99%, RoboTwin 83.8% to 91.4% over ten iterations. It was the only method among Best-of-N, filtered SFT, IBRL, DSRL and DAWR that improved stably from failures without an auxiliary actor. That is a direct argument for capturing and labeling failures, not just clean demos, and it prices a category most collection contracts currently throw away. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21204)
- **SCAPE (arXiv 2608.19425) treats policy evaluation as a standalone product, cutting scenario-level prediction error by 4.9% and 34.7% against neural and statistical baselines in driving and by 14.5% and 27.7% on a quadruped, validated on a physical Unitree Go2.** It corrects sim-to-real bias in simulation labels before fitting, then calibrates with conformal prediction, and argues population averages obscure scenario-specific variation and say little about where a policy can safely deploy. Predicting per-scenario real performance from mostly-simulated rollouts plus a few real ones is the validation half of a data business. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19425)
- **EXL completed its acquisition of iMerit for up to $310M, structured as $170M upfront plus $140M in earnouts over two years, with founder Radha Ramaswami Basu joining EXL as EVP.** The assets are the Ango Hub multimodal annotation platform and the Scholars expert network, and iMerit's verticals include autonomous systems and robotics. The deal was announced in July and reported complete in August, and I could not pin the exact completion date from a primary source, so treat the timing as reported rather than verified. This is the comp and the exit path for mid-tier annotation vendors, and it removes one more independent physical-AI annotation buyer from the field. [EXL](https://www.exlservice.com/about/newsroom/exl-to-acquire-imerit-advancing-its-leadership-in-enterprise-ai-by-adding-foundation-model-expertise-and-technology)
- **HireArt argued on August 22 that the binding constraint in physical AI is the workforce rather than the robots, and named the role taxonomy explicitly: robot operators, field technicians, teleoperators, QA validators and data capture specialists.** It observes roughly an even split between fixed W-2 core and variable surge capacity, and warns that speed-only metrics actively degrade performance in physical environments. An independent third party naming data capture specialist and QA validator as first-class job families is external validation for the position, and the accountability-over-throughput point is a pricing argument against gig-style competitors. [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/robots-dont-run-themselves-workforce-powering-physical-ai/)
- **Surge AI scored Qwen 3.8 Max at 58.7 on its Tuesday Work Index, up 8.6 points from Qwen 3.7 Max, and put it on the ComplexConstraints cost-performance Pareto frontier.** First model-scored follow-up since the index launched, which tells you Surge is running it as a live leaderboard cadence rather than a one-off. A Chinese open-weights model closing to within about 8 points of Fable 5 on professional work is the secondary read. [Surge AI](https://surgehq.ai/blog/qwen-3-8-max-scores-58-7-on-the-tuesday-work-index)
- **Logic-VLA (arXiv 2608.20556) improves Signal Temporal Logic satisfaction by 24.8 to 40.7 percentage points over an STL-blind base policy while costing at most 1.8 points of nominal natural-language task success, and generalizes to formulas unseen in training.** Natural language cannot express safety-critical or spatiotemporal constraints. If formal specs become the interface, demonstrations need STL-style structured annotation, which is a higher-margin labeling product than plain language captions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20556)
- **Two token-compression papers land on the same idea from different ends. RoleSub (arXiv 2608.18410) compresses total KV cache to 9.2% to 11.3% of original and beats a token-only control in 33 of 36 settings on OpenVLA-OFT-7B across all four LIBERO suites.** Action-JND (arXiv 2608.21247) defines compressibility by how much a token can change before the action deviates unacceptably, rather than by attention or saliency proxies, though its abstract carries no numbers. The second framing is the more useful one for us: action tolerance is also a rule for deciding which frames of ego data are worth annotating. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18410), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21247)

### Embedded AI systems

- **GS-VLA (arXiv 2608.19066) reports that a small displacement of the camera mount drops LIBERO success from about 90% to about 10% in the worst case, and fixes it with a 4M-parameter 3D Gaussian canonicalizer prepended to a frozen policy.** No retraining, no catastrophic forgetting, and it improves across architectures, unseen task suites and perturbation scales. That 90 to 10 number is the most quotable result of the window and it makes camera-rig consistency a hard requirement on any capture hardware spec. Mount tolerance is a data-quality spec, not a mechanical detail. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19066)
- **ForeTime-VLA (arXiv 2608.20735) distills a frozen world-model teacher into 64-dimensional future tokens so the world model never runs at deployment, reaching 81.1% stationary and 58.9% slow-moving grasp success on a real conveyor, beating the next best by 12.2 and 22.2 points.** Across three belt speeds it took 44 of 90 grasps against 23 of 90 for pi-0.5, including 11 of 30 versus 2 of 30 at fast speed, at a latency cost of 2.46% to 2.93%. Note the honest gap: the offline metric moved only about 3% while the real-robot gain was large, so offline evaluation understates the effect here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20735)
- **RP1 (arXiv 2608.18669) uses 1,000x fewer world-model rollouts and runs up to 67x faster than the strongest alternative under concurrent planner inference, while beating hand-designed search across visual navigation, arm reaching and manipulation on two world-model backbones.** It trains offline from imagined rollouts and attaches to any pretrained latent world model. Three orders of magnitude off the rollout budget is what moves world-model planning toward something an embedded controller can actually run. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18669)
- **DECOWAM (arXiv 2608.20114) cuts action MSE by 21.7% over FastWAM using 25.95M trainable adaptation parameters on a frozen backbone, and releases ARMDOG, a real-robot dataset synchronizing video, whole-body state, action and language over 79 closed-loop trials per method.** The reason to read it is that it explicitly separates camera ego-motion from base and arm actions, because world-action models built for fixed-base platforms conflate them. That is the same problem head-mounted capture has, and if the disentanglement is what makes world models work under a moving viewpoint, then the disentanglement is an annotation deliverable. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20114)
- **GhostTac (arXiv 2608.20817) demonstrates the first contactless attack on tactile sensing, using crafted electromagnetic interference that becomes persistent DC offsets and bypasses onboard filtering, validated across 10 sensor modules, two dexterous hands and 15 tactile sensors of different types.** It can induce excessive force that may damage objects or injure people. If tactile streams can be silently biased without contact, sensor-level provenance becomes part of what a data vendor certifies, alongside label accuracy. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20817)
- **Schaeffler completed validation and will mass-produce formed strain wave gearboxes for humanoids in Germany in 2027, with forming rather than precision machining cutting manufacturing cost by more than 25% and material consumption by more than 75%, taking key steps from minutes to seconds.** Actuators are roughly half the bill of materials of a humanoid, and Schaeffler has shipped more than 2 million formed strain wave gearboxes into automotive over ten years. This is the humanoid unit-cost curve moving via a supplier with real volume behind it. [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/schaeffler-plans-to-mass-manufacture-gearboxes-for-humanoids-in-2027/)

### Robotics & humanoids

- **GEN-1.5 is the strongest single capability result of the window.** 59% average success across 10 diverse tasks one-shot in-context with zero gradient updates from 3 to 12 seconds of one demonstration, 83% after 10 gradient steps on about 50 demos, 66.5% on a held-out task after one gradient step on one minute of data. It ingests 30 seconds of video memory and outputs 100 Hz action trajectories, was pretrained continuously for 8+ months, and its nearest-neighbour search ran over 1,891,392 scenes. It also does zero-shot sim-to-real via physical prompting with no sim data in pretraining at all. The read for us cuts both ways: human demonstration data becomes directly promptable without training, which raises the value of ego capture, while the amount needed per task collapses, which moves the premium to breadth and provenance of the pretraining corpus. Evan Ackerman's caveat is the one to hold onto, that Generalist can only claim no relevant pretraining data to the best of their knowledge, which is precisely the provenance gap a validation vendor sells against. [Generalist AI](https://generalistai.com/blog/gen-1.5), [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/video-friday-unitree-superhuman)
- **HiTac-WAM (arXiv 2608.19574) roughly doubles contact-rich real-robot success, from 31.1% to 61.1% with forecast-guided action-chunk selection and 72.2% with the full system, across chip grasping, blackboard erasing and USB insertion.** Mean contact F1 is 0.921, the directed hierarchy cuts 3D displacement L2 error by 17.6% against a deformation-only predictor, and improves slip AUPRC by 60.4% against a slip-only predictor. Predicting touch before acting is what buys the gain, and the hierarchy implies tactile streams need capturing and labeling as contact state, then deformation field, then slip risk, rather than dumped as raw images. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574)
- **ViTacPhys (arXiv 2608.21355) extracts mass, friction and stiffness from ordinary human visual-tactile demonstrations across 60 rigid and deformable objects, hitting 97.2% mass and 98.8% friction classification with 5.51% stiffness MAPE on seen objects, and 87.5%, 97.5% and 9.08% on held-out objects from known categories.** Transferred human to robot with limited teleoperation data, the conditioned policy reaches 95.0% grasp success in-distribution and 83.4% out. Physical properties are a new label class derivable from wearable capture, and they transfer with only a little teleop. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21355)
- **The Missing Touch (arXiv 2608.19372) quantifies how much a missing feedback channel corrupts the demonstrations you sell, reducing deviation between teleoperated and natural human trajectories by 29% to 79% using a 2-DoF bilateral force-feedback telemanipulator with a 32-DoF tactile fingertip display.** Higher tactile resolution compresses the state-space distribution of teleoperated motions, which the authors link to better downstream policy training. This is the sharpest available argument that rig quality determines dataset quality, and it implies buyers should be asking about feedback fidelity rather than only hours. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19372)

### Twitter / X signal

*Ninth consecutive run without browser X access, and this time the failure was clean and diagnosable. The extension reported one connected browser and `select_browser` succeeded twice, but `tabs_context_mcp` timed out on every attempt across five full retry cycles, including after a fresh `select_browser` and a standalone `navigate`. The signature, connected extension plus a tool that never responds, points at a pending permission prompt sitting unanswered in the extension side panel rather than a network fault. That is worth one manual check, because it may be a thirty-second fix.*

*The fallback found a workaround worth keeping. X posts can be read and precisely dated without authentication via `https://cdn.syndication.twimg.com/tweet-result?id=<ID>&lang=en&token=a`, which returns full text, handle and exact `created_at`, and any post can be dated from its ID alone using the Snowflake epoch. The bottleneck is discovering IDs, not reading them, and the only reliable discovery route found was scraping tweet embeds out of robotics news pages. Nitter, xcancel, lightbrd, the syndication timeline endpoint, Bing and Brave all failed or rate-limited.*

### Research papers worth a look

- **DreamHand** (arXiv [2608.20308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20308)). Occlusion-robust egocentric 3D hand recovery, 30% and 40% error cuts on ARCTIC and HOT3D, no test-time intrinsics needed.
- **Beyond Imitation** (arXiv [2608.21204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21204)). Off-policy Q-planning turns failed rollouts into gains, real-robot 40% to 90% and 25% to 80% with frozen BC weights.
- **GS-VLA** (arXiv [2608.19066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19066)). A small camera-mount shift drops LIBERO success from about 90% to about 10%, fixed by a 4M-parameter canonicalizer on a frozen policy.
- **Rethinking Demonstration Unlearning** (arXiv [2608.20784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20784)). Behavior and evidence axes of demonstration deletion dissociate in both directions, with a conformal joint test.
- **HiTac-WAM** (arXiv [2608.19574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574)). Hierarchical tactile world-action model, real-robot contact-rich success 31.1% to 72.2%, contact F1 0.921.
- **ViTacPhys** (arXiv [2608.21355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21355)). Mass, friction and stiffness recovered from human visual-tactile demos, 97.2% and 98.8% classification, 95.0% grasp success.
- **What Matters for Latent Actions** (arXiv [2608.19613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19613)). 41 design choices for Latent Action Models under one framework, the reference on turning unlabeled human video into policy signal.
- **Koala Gripper** (arXiv [2608.20546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20546)). Co-designing the human capture device and the robot gripper, from the Robotics and AI Institute. No success numbers in the abstract.
- **EgoHRV** (arXiv [2608.18711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18711)). Heart-rate variability from headset gaze cameras with no added sensor, 17.8% accuracy lift on EgoExo4D proficiency estimation.
- **SCAPE** (arXiv [2608.19425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19425)). Scenario-conditioned policy evaluation, 34.7% and 27.7% error reductions, validated on a physical Unitree Go2.
- **ForeTime-VLA** (arXiv [2608.20735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20735)). Future-token distillation so the world model never runs at deployment, 81.1% stationary conveyor grasp success at under 3% latency cost.
- **RP1** (arXiv [2608.18669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18669)). Reinforced planning with latent world models, 1,000x fewer rollouts and up to 67x faster.
- **DECOWAM** (arXiv [2608.20114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20114)). Decoupled whole-body world-action model separating camera ego-motion from base and arm actions, plus the ARMDOG dataset.
- **RoleSub** (arXiv [2608.18410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18410)). Role-conditioned sub-token routing cuts VLA KV cache to about 10% of original, winning 33 of 36 matched-budget settings.
- **Action-JND** (arXiv [2608.21247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21247)). Token compressibility defined by action tolerance rather than saliency. No numbers in the abstract.
- **Logic-VLA** (arXiv [2608.20556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20556)). Temporal-logic conditioning, 24.8 to 40.7 point STL satisfaction gain at up to 1.8 points of nominal success.
- **The Missing Touch** (arXiv [2608.19372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19372)). Spatially distributed tactile feedback cuts teleop-to-human trajectory deviation by 29% to 79%.
- **GhostTac** (arXiv [2608.20817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20817)). Contactless electromagnetic attack on tactile sensors across 10 modules and 15 sensors, bypassing onboard filtering.
- **Grounded-Exo2Ego** (arXiv [2608.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20534)). Exo-to-ego generation with a re-localization fix for camera-reconstruction misalignment. Claims large margins, gives no numbers.
- **Video2DoorTraversal** (arXiv [2608.20251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20251)). One RGB video per environment, 96.57% real-door success and 80.95% zero-shot on unseen doors, all inference onboard.
- **VLMs for Egocentric Video** (arXiv [2608.18671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18671)). Survey finding models read visible objects far better than evolving interactions and intent.
- **Inter-X++** (arXiv [2608.20312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20312)). Human-human interaction benchmark, 11,388 sequences and 8.1M+ frames with vertex-level contact maps.
- **GOAG** (arXiv [2608.19759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19759)). Object-agnostic dexterous grasping at 86.93% on MultiDex, matching object-specific training while needing none.
- **Natural Sit-to-Stand** (arXiv [2608.20823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20823)). Above 97% success across eight chair heights learned from scratch with no demonstrations or reference trajectories.
- **AdaPT** (arXiv [2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20087)). Professional tennis serve and rally styles learned from broadcast video, deployed on Unitree G1 and a 1.7m Dobot Atom without motion capture.
- **HarvestPoint-ACT** (arXiv [2608.18446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18446)). 88% harvest success and 75% under heavy occlusion by making target and grip point explicit 8-D state instead of inferring from pixels.
- **MLREF** (arXiv [2608.18827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18827)). LLM-driven modular reward design, 25.2% locomotion and 6.6% manipulation gains across 17 tasks via a reusable module pool.
