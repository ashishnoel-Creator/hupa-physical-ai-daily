    Data collection

    October 1 · SoTa supplies a shared human/robot tactile layout. Its conformable skin has 202 taxels, with materials below $10 per skin; that is not a complete instrumented-glove price. Human and robot demonstrations share an encoder without a learned sensor mapping. With a fixed robot-data budget, human examples raise mean success from 22.8% to 45.9% across eight conditions. Fabrication resources are promised, not established as released. Paper

    October 1 · NEEDLEWORK preserves useful parts of failed recordings. Short verified action bridges connect observations using RGB, proprioception and episode outcomes, without new environment interaction. Original demonstrations remain in training. The reported real-robot gain averages 21 percentage points over each task’s strongest baseline. Operational implication: retain outcomes and recoverable failure segments instead of treating every failed episode as waste. Paper

    October 1 · MoRoOp presents an operational record, not just camera footage. Nine eight-hour laboratory shifts yield 1,382 jobs, 4,815 operations, 19,352 dispatch events and 140,386 robot-state observations. Delays, obstructed navigation and unsuccessful operations are retained, with raw and cleaned state tables. It is a laboratory logistics dataset, not a production-factory census. Dataset paper

    October 2 · DexJoCo-X controls the comparison across robot hands. Seven hands, six tasks and 2,100 balanced demonstrations share scenes, outcomes and execution interfaces. Results show that an action representation’s usefulness depends on pretraining and architecture; simply expanding an action head is insufficient. Paper

    October 2 · AdaTempo and SARI reduce different collection costs. AdaTempo retimes demonstrations by task phase, reporting up to 3.57× speedup, not a universal throughput gain. SARI collects physical contact phases and simulates diverse approaches: collection time falls 34.3% in five tasks, but unseen-placement success is still 27.5%. AdaTempo, SARI

    Data annotation & validation

    October 1 · CriticHack shows why rising reward and success can conceal wrong-object errors. Across the reported drawer-task runs, task success increases 10.2 points while wrong-object failures increase 10.9 points. A frozen outcome verifier redirects optimisation. The study argues for explicit error-type checks alongside aggregate success. Paper

    October 2 · ManiPhysicsBench measures whether success damages the object. Geometry, material properties and grasp conditions define solver-based damage thresholds. Continuous object-specific gripper labels improve preservation, but reduce ordinary task success and generalise imperfectly. A completed task and an undamaged object are separate acceptance criteria. Paper

    October 2 · Dense instruction labels connect slower reasoning to fast local control. A study combines autonomously generated demonstrations with corrective segments and language supervision at primitive, atomic and composite levels. Its long-horizon evaluations test whether local policies keep following changing instructions. This is technical evidence for richer labels, not a disclosed annotation contract. Paper

    Embedded AI systems

    October 2 · HexVIO moves the visual frontend onto a commodity Hexagon DSP. Stereo-inertial tracking retains a CPU backend and reports 30 FPS at 0.83 W, equivalent to about 18 hours on the test device. The reported 67% power reduction and 86% throughput increase are evaluated configurations, not a guaranteed all-device improvement. Paper

    October 2 · FastOPD trades some task performance for lower latency. Its two-step distilled policy retains 84% of pi-0.5 performance on LIBERO while reducing inference latency 78.1%. The paper also evaluates other teachers and a compact real-robot student. Report the performance loss as well as the latency saving. Paper

    October 2 · LLA-MPC adapts dynamics on embedded hardware. F1TENTH experiments evaluate thousands of candidate models online to identify changing tyre parameters. Low-friction tests complete tracking tasks where a fixed model fails; this is adaptive control evidence rather than a new foundation model. Paper

    Robotics & humanoids

    October 2 · EyeRobot 2.0 tests active gaze instead of wrist cameras. It uses one swivelling stereo camera, fixation-relative actions and concentrated visual tokens. Across over 1,000 physical trials, it reports 69% versus 64% success against an ego-plus-wrist policy when wrist views are clear, and 48% versus 22% when grasped objects occlude them. These are camera-condition comparisons, not universal replacement of wrist sensing. Paper

    October 2 · SimpleTouch adds touch during task fine-tuning. A tactile expert uses a frozen pretrained tactile encoder and task demonstrations, without extra tactile-policy pretraining. It reports 71.3% average across four real tasks. This still relies on pretrained representations and instrumented demonstrations. Paper

    October 2 · Reflex policies and MobiAgent target recovery at different levels. A frozen proprioceptive execution layer improves contact response, with peg-insertion success still 36–58%. MobiAgent segments and verifies rollouts to extend a skill library, reporting Astribot S1 success rising 32.5% to 57.5% through data recycling. Both retain meaningful failure rates. Reflex policy, MobiAgent

    Research papers worth a look

    Monday’s cs.RO list contained 59 new submissions and 123 entries including cross-lists and replacements. The following world-model/simulation studies complement the papers above. Dates are original submissions; all results remain bounded by their reported experiments.

    Submitted Paper Useful finding and limit
    October 1 Awomo-SimDataEngine Validates assets/scenes and repairs failures before generating replayable robot demonstrations. Cross-simulator co-training improves LIBERO-Plus from 77.17% to 89.43%, with smaller long-horizon gains.
    October 1 ViGAR Separates visual-subgoal planning from action execution, with shared world-model representations; physical tests cover five compositional and two in-context tasks.
    October 1 PEWAM Compiles demonstrations into object effects and contact points, separating them from a particular arm. Real Franka tests use prior fine-tuning; single-video conditioning is not zero-data robot deployment.
    October 2 DeltaWorld Predicts action-induced latent changes and supervises interaction regions. Better video metrics alone do not establish improved physical policy performance.
    October 2 PointWAM Forecasts scene and hand point trajectories jointly, supporting human-video pretraining and robot retargeting. Scene supervision adds value beyond hand motion alone in the evaluated tasks.
    October 2 Skill2Real Verifies and governs executable skills through a shared API, then transfers frozen skill memories. Four physical tasks reach 78.75% mean completion; embodiment interfaces still matter.
    October 2 I2CD Predicts collision-ready convex geometry directly from images. A 20-object scene takes 11 seconds versus 328 for a baseline; physical pick/place is 85/100 versus 90/100.
    October 2 XGenAct Jointly models RGB, actions, depth, normals and functional segmentation through video codecs. Evaluation is on held-out RLBench tasks, not field deployments.
    October 2 RATE Adds task-conditioned alert supervision and interaction history to tactile features; risk labels are a distinct supervision layer.
    October 2 AVL-JEPA Anchors latent dynamics to executed actions so visual invariance does not erase action consequences; evaluated on four robotic-control tasks.
