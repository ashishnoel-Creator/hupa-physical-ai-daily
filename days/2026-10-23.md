### Data collection

**Catch-up, October 19 — Dream-1 changes the route from demonstrations to deployment.** The [technical release](https://dreamlabs.ai/research/dream-1/) describes a world-action model for bimanual robots with parallel grippers, using a head view and two wrist views. Current listed model usage is $3/hour for Dream-1 and $2/hour for Dream-1-flash; these are inference prices, not data rates or total operating cost. Fine-tuning examples use each robot's own data, with duration varying by embodiment. The model predicts end-effector targets and depends on the robot's inverse-kinematics solver. It is a research beta with material generalisation limits. **Inference:** task-specific demonstrations, failure cases and adaptation evaluation remain relevant even when zero-shot transfer improves; the announcement does not disclose a supplier buying mandate.

**Catch-up, October 21 — Lattice's sample separates captured and derived views.** Its multiview reconstruction produces virtual egocentric views without a head-mounted camera. These should not be described as equivalent to native headcam recordings. The public sample includes ball handling and shirt folding with LeRobot-compatible episodes; the accessible article does not establish a commercial dataset licence. The linked dataset card could not be recovered in this pass. [Publisher article](https://huggingface.co/blog/LatticeAdmin/sample-4d-dataset).

**Research:** EgoHand combines 14M images from synthetic, in-the-wild and multiview sources for monocular 3D hands. LocoManiLab supplies 234 simulation tasks and 51.5 hours of simulated teleoperation, with three matched real-world tasks; it is not a 234-task physical capture campaign. PATH studies operator preferences from 15 operators and 225 trajectories. Original abstracts and limitations appear in the research table.

### Data annotation & validation

**October 22 — physical-event labels become a sensor output.** Robotiq's [2FC platform](https://robotiq.com/physical-ai-grippers) exposes pressure, vibration and gripper-state streams with millisecond timestamps and automatic event labels. Its contact software reports grip quality, slip and related states. Model-generated labels still need validation against task ground truth; vendor output is not independent acceptance evidence.

**Research priority:** RGraph-Eval checks generated robot videos through task-grounded scene graphs; SCOPE uses plan errors to revise scene graphs; Echo trains on success, failure and recovery trajectories; Progress Probes estimates runtime progress from policy activations. These support retaining failures and rework context in datasets instead of delivering only visually successful clips. Sensorimotor Alignment is an offline evaluation proxy, not a replacement for closed-loop trials. See the original papers below.
