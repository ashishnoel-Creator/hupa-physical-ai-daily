### Data collection

**October 23 — DexPort is directly relevant to dexterous capture design.** The operator moves a handheld robot hand and selects coordinated skills instead of controlling every joint. The same hand is used for collection and deployment, recording commanded targets as well as measured states. Authors report over 2,500 demonstrations across four tasks, including in-the-wild collection without a robot arm. It is a hardware-specific alternative to retargeting human motion, not proof that ordinary POV video contains equivalent action labels. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32969)

**October 22 — EgoForesight treats source reliability as part of learning.** It combines high-quality robot trajectories with noisier reconstructed egocentric actions and supervision from future outcomes. Reported gains at 30,000 pretraining hours support distinguishing capture quality and label reliability instead of treating every recorded hour identically. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31845)

**October 23 — collection diversity, pace and cooperation:** [DiscoDemo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32093) uses diverse successful RL trajectories to train imitation policies. [Learned speed priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32676) separate task motion from teleoperator timing. [CoCarry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32460) contributes 220 synchronized two-person carrying sequences across 21 task schemes, five object geometries and four terrains. These suggest distinct data products; none is a paid collection request.

No independently verified release-specific episode total or task-success improvement is asserted. [Official announcement](https://www.agibot.com/article/231/detail/129.html)

**October 22–23 — label more than success/failure.** [KiRoSe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31801) uses kinematic events for temporal segmentation and reports transfer to unseen embodiments and egocentric human motion. [NimCritic / LOOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32776) combine automatic labels with human calibration: over 800 training-video hours and 19,618 evaluation frames for task progress. The newly available [FLORA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31779) documents the previously covered caption/reward engine; its large-scale throughput and cost comparisons remain author-reported.

**Failure evidence matters:** [OpenVLA feature analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32208) predicts failure-associated behaviours; [Backtrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32941) trains a small outcome head to trigger recovery for frozen VLAs. Conversely, [TTT with human demonstrations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32094) reports no consistent demonstration-conditioned target selection in its tested settings. [Human corrections in federated adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32888) is explicitly an evaluation agenda, not experimental evidence.

**Training-system caution:** [The bf16 VLA audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31859) reports that small updates can round away without fp32 master weights. Before attributing poor fine-tuning to collected data, verify the training configuration. This is a finding from specified implementations and benchmarks, not a claim about every VLA stack.
