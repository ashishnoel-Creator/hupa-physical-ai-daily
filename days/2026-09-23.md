### Data collection

- **September 17 · Figure gives the human-data thesis a useful controlled comparison.** Helix 2.5 was tested on tidying, towel folding and bed making in 30 previously unseen homes. Figure says it held downstream data, architecture, training and evaluation fixed and changed only Index pretraining: success went from 9% to 56%. Success required finishing the full task; safety interventions counted as failures. “Zero-shot” means unseen homes and objects, not unseen tasks or no robot fine-tuning. This is company-reported evidence, and 56% is still a substantial distance from dependable household labour. [Technical release](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix-2-5-zero-shot-30-home-generalization)
- **September 15 · XPENG's XPACE explains what sits between human video and robot control.** Its 5,000-hour training collection has four layers: broad ego video, human video paired with actions, task- and appearance-aligned bridge data, and IRON teleoperation. Episode goals and local action captions share a representation with human and robot poses. Failed trajectories train the simulator, with their actions excluded from imitation. A later policy stage mixes 8% synthetic recovery examples into the original recipe and reports success increasing from 61.7% to 86.7% on three tasks. That before/after result also includes extra training; it does not isolate a universal recovery-data effect. The practical lesson is to specify the bridge and the intended use of failures when designing a collection. [Project and evaluation](https://xpeng-robotics.github.io/xpace/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17372)
- **September 20 · EgoWild2Dex targets ordinary environments and dexterous transfer.** The authors describe 538.9 hours of egocentric video and 179,049 episodes from homes, factories and pharmacies, with language and object annotations. Their results include 96.7% on three long-horizon tasks but only 33.3% in an object zero-shot setting. Those are different tests, not competing estimates of one universal success rate. Data, code and models are promised for release; this run did not verify downloadable assets. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23755)
- **September 21, revised September 22 · Zeva-Ego adds an explicit human/robot data comparison.** On RoboTwin, 10,000 hours of ego data increased success from 63.8% to 75.3%, compared with 74.7% using 2,000 hours of robot demonstrations. The authors describe a roughly 4–5:1 empirical ratio in that setup. Treat it as a study-specific result, not a market exchange rate for data. Its separate adaptation experiment also improves with repeated attempts, so it should not be quoted as first-attempt reliability. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24411)

### Data annotation & validation

- **September 22 · Snorkel is raising around research and evaluation, not just annotation capacity.** The company announced a $350M Series E at a $3.5B valuation, led by Insight and S32. Its stated direction is expert-and-model collaboration to create difficult training tasks, environments and evaluations. This is a broad AI-data business, not a robotics revenue comparable. The relevant signal is that buyers increasingly need suppliers to diagnose model weaknesses and produce data against them. [Company announcement](https://snorkel.ai/blog/data-2-0-and-the-research-era-of-ai-data/)
- **September 16 · Voxel51's EMG audit is a useful warning about sensor labels.** Its analysis of eight episodes, one wearer and one session found weak agreement between wrist EMG and a vision-derived finger-force estimate; the strongest correlation was 0.426 and best lags ranged from −230 to +200 milliseconds. Despite loose wording in the article, the force channel is an estimate from pose and depth, not physically measured ground truth. This does not establish that EMG cannot predict force. It shows why channel provenance, per-session calibration and timing checks belong in a multimodal capture specification. [Reproducible analysis](https://voxel51.com/blog/egocentric-emg-force-dataset-fiftyone)
- **September 21 · H2RBench makes human-to-robot transfer comparisons less arbitrary.** Four manipulation tasks use real human demonstrations and a common simulation evaluation protocol. Across the tested method/task combinations, simulation and real-robot performance had a reported Pearson correlation of 0.89. Useful as a screening method before expensive hardware tests, with the important limit that four tasks do not validate every environment or embodiment. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24778)
- **September 21–22 · Two evaluations show why clean benchmark scores are insufficient.** LIBERO-VPro tests stale frames, missing visual information, inconsistent sources and changed scene conditions, using roughly 196,000 simulated episodes plus 200 real rollouts. IndustrialVLA-Bench finds much larger separation on robustness and instruction paraphrases than on clean LIBERO scores, while explicitly separating faithful reproductions from weaker evidence. For a buyer acceptance test, include changed conditions, recovery, runtime and evidence quality. A leaderboard average will miss these differences. [LIBERO-VPro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24350), [IndustrialVLA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25562)

### Embedded AI systems

- **September 22 · Isaac ROS 5.0 is available.** NVIDIA's release adds ROS Lyrical/Ubuntu 24.04 support, development workflows for AI coding agents, and accelerated robotics capabilities across Jetson hardware. Its contribution with the Open Source Robotics Alliance also targets a standard, vendor-neutral interface for accelerated memory transport. This is practical infrastructure for perception and manipulation development, not a new general-purpose robot policy. [Release](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/isaac-ros-5-0-agentic-open-source-robotics/)
- **September 22 · Intrinsic Core opens a substantial part of the industrial software stack.** The Apache-2.0 release includes ROS-compatible services, hardware-agnostic real-time control, asset models and a digital twin. The accompanying Open Machine Tending Solution is a reference application that supports customisation across hardware and perception models, including FoundationPose. It reduces the amount an integrator must build before testing a use case. It does not remove commissioning, safety or application engineering. [Release](https://www.intrinsic.ai/blog/posts/introducing-intrinsic-core), [Core repository](https://github.com/intrinsic-ai/intrinsic-core), [machine-tending repository](https://github.com/intrinsic-ai/intrinsic-omts)
- **September 21 · VLAQuantBench says quantisation must be tested in the control loop.** Across 409 runs and 94,574 simulated episodes, low-precision behaviour depended on the model, layer selection and calibration recipe. A fix that helped one policy could harm another. Do not assume a model that fits into edge memory retains its manipulation performance; validate the deployed numerical configuration on actual tasks. The study also includes physical-robot and real-kernel measurements. [Paper and code links](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25376)
- **September 21 · World-model knowledge can be distilled without running the world model at deployment.** “Think Like a World Model, Act Like a VLA” aligns a compact policy to cached teacher features. The authors report a 0.8B policy at 32 milliseconds and 1.86GB on an RTX 5090, with simulation and real-hardware evaluations. This is an interesting route to cheaper inference; those desktop GPU measurements are not a Jetson performance claim. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24682)
- **September 21 · NVIDIA's Halos update emphasises independent safety layers.** The engineering point is to separate learned autonomy from hardware and software mechanisms that can monitor or stop it. Read alongside Digit 5's independent safety controller. This is a current technical explanation of the stack, not a claim that Halos itself first launched this week. [Technical overview](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/physical-ai-halos-safety/)

### Research papers worth a look

**Read first for human-data strategy:** [XPACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17372) (September 15), [EgoWild2Dex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23755) (September 20), [Zeva-Ego](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24411) and [H2RBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24778) (both September 21). Together they cover data composition, real-world collection, transfer efficiency and comparison methodology. Their methods and benchmarks differ; their headline success rates should not be ranked against one another.

**Read next for evaluation and deployment:** [LIBERO-VPro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24350), [VLAQuantBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25376), [world-model feature distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24682) (September 21), and [IndustrialVLA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25562) (September 22). These address visual failure modes, numerical precision, inference cost and reproducible comparisons.

Two further papers deserve attention:

- **September 22 · PhyVisGen:** combines soft-contact simulation with reconstructed scenes and realistic rendering. Policies trained on synthetic demonstrations report 65–95% success across five real-robot tasks without real-robot demonstrations or policy fine-tuning. Promising evidence for targeted synthetic augmentation, not a finding that all real data can be replaced. The paper is under review. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25653)
- **September 16 · GAVEL:** explicit state graphs, preconditions and repair substantially improve the evaluated language-model planners on BEHAVIOR-1K tasks. Its relevance is making state and task validity explicit. These are planning/simulation results, not measured uptime on physical robots. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19315)

Also retained: [Uranus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24815), the simulator release described above. Paper summaries reflect author-reported evidence; experiments were not reproduced in this run. Availability of release links does not establish their licence or suitability for commercial use.

### What this changes for an egocentric-data business

**Second-pass opportunity check, September 23:** three public partnership signals deserved attention alongside the model news. These routes were checked today; their original launch dates are not established within September 11–23.

- **DeepReach has an explicit local data-partner programme.** It offers a wearable capture device and an upload/payment workflow. Its example of about $7 per qualified hour is illustrative, task-dependent and not guaranteed. Device terms, local task availability, rejection rates and net margins require confirmation. [Partner programme](https://www.deepreach.ai/join)
- **Generalist describes external vendor sourcing, trials and commercial terms.** Its Data Partnerships job listing discusses finding collection partners, providing hardware, expanding geographies and measuring cost per usable unit. This is direct evidence of external partnership activity, although a hiring page is not a public supplier tender. [Company job description](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/generalist/d5aa97c1-fc3e-439a-9aba-b8c9aaa2dab4)
- **Praxis invites physical-work businesses to participate.** Its founder announcement describes workplace demonstrations and explicitly invites businesses in logistics, manufacturing, food, retail and labs to discuss monetising their activities. This is a route to investigate, not an assurance of an available contract. Exact launch-date metadata is inconsistent, so it is recorded as a current discovery. [Founder announcement](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/STf-praxis-ai-training-data-for-robotics)

**Interpretation, rather than additional news:** the window strengthens the argument for human demonstrations as a training input. It also makes a generic “hours of video” proposition harder to defend. The evidence points toward a more specific offer: identifiable tasks and environments, useful variation, reliable sensor provenance, and a test showing what the buyer's policy gains.

Three questions now matter in a buyer conversation:

1. **Where does this collection enter the training recipe?** Broad pretraining, task-aligned bridging, imitation and simulator failure modelling need different data. XPACE makes that distinction concrete.
2. **What proves the data helped?** Define held-out objects, sites and tasks, a fixed comparison, intervention treatment and an agreed completion rubric. Figure and H2RBench are useful references, without treating their results as guarantees.
3. **Which information cannot be recovered from the video later?** Contact/force provenance, calibration, synchronisation and meaningful failure annotations need deliberate collection choices. The EMG audit illustrates why a labelled channel can still be unsuitable as ground truth.

The most useful immediate reading order is Figure's evaluation, XPACE's data design, H2RBench and the Intrinsic Core release. They cover the evidence for demand, the data product, its evaluation and the surrounding deployment infrastructure.
