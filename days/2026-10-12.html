    Data collection

    October 8: PanTact adds the missing body-contact channel. Wearable modular patches supplement ego vision and bimanual UMI grippers. A policy predicts contact goals and a controller realizes them on Unitree G1 without extra robot body-contact sensors. This expands demonstrations to support, pushing, sweeping and load sharing. It does not establish a commercial capture standard. Paper

    October 9: Human2Dex separates reusable human data from robot-specific labels. A wrist-centric corpus transfers to Linker O6 and Wuji through calibrated representations and retargeting. Reported success is 90.8% and 80.8% over six tasks; checkpoints and action labels remain hand-specific. Reusability is conditional, not plug-and-play across arbitrary hands. Paper

    Timing and occlusion are part of the specification. Chronos uses observation-capture and desired execution timestamps for asynchronous data. EgoReveal learns to reposition the view before manipulating occluded objects. Ego/IMU analysis warns that video embeddings can be dominated by where the head points. These motivate modality and synchronization QA rather than an hours-only rubric.

    Synthetic stereo changes the economics, but does not settle quality equivalence. StereoEngine generates stereo pairs from monocular demonstrations or simulation and reports bimanual policy gains. It is not proof that synthetic stereo satisfies BARG’s unshared acceptance rubric. Splat2Act uses phone scene scans and object images to create over 68,000 simulated pickup demonstrations across 101 captured environments; its real Stretch 3 result is limited to a 12-task subset.

    Data annotation & validation

    Benchmark the reason for failure and the value of intervention. Spa-Bench releases 1,200 teleoperated demonstrations and 2,892 evaluation rollouts, exposing spatial-grounding gaps despite familiar objects/actions. RESCUE uses 128,340 simulated counterfactual branches to show that accurate alarms do not necessarily reduce intervention cost. Its 2.0–3.3% false-alarm tolerance belongs to a particular stop/reset model, not a universal operational threshold.

    Scene flow and failure evidence are becoming deliverables. MeshBind combines six surround cameras with LiDAR, a correction tool and a 41,000-frame non-rigid scene-flow benchmark. Robot-WATCHDOG evaluates object-level semantic failures. JitterVLA separates controller-induced jerk from policy quality; its code/data are promised, not confirmed downloadable here.

    Embedded AI systems

    SmolWAM, October 9: reports 57 ms per 16-action chunk and 2.4 GiB GPU memory, with 95.5% success across 400 LIBERO episodes. This is a compact inference result; the abstract does not establish those numbers on every edge device or a real production robot. Paper

    BT-Agent, October 9: runs a 9B model onboard a quadruped while behavior-tree checks can stop navigation without waiting for the next model decision. Its text/simulation comparisons and five physical task categories provide different levels of evidence. Paper

    Evo-World’s lightweight motion supervision and online tactile control are included below. NVIDIA/Qualcomm searches mainly resurfaced older releases; no additional fresh chip announcement was verified.

    Robotics & humanoids

    Reflex, October 8: real G1 box-catching success is reported at 65% from 2.5 m and 40% from 5 m, below human baselines. Egocentric RGB-D history helps recover moving-object dynamics. Paper

    Contact preservation links human video to robot execution. VICAR transfers ten third-person interaction skills while retaining contact timing/poses; PriMoRetarget prioritizes interaction constraints. ATHLETE combines existing locomotion data and tennis videos for reactive rallies. These are bounded research demonstrations, not general task competence.

    AlphaNIST learns all 20 assembly-board tasks with binary rewards in simulation. It should not be reported as demonstration-free real-world deployment. Further memory, adaptation and motion papers are listed below.

    World models & sim-to-real

    Three distinct directions emerge: LeWAM jointly learns latent dynamics and actions; Forward Dynamics Models use future-state supervision while skipping future generation at inference; Grounded-Dreamer checks synthetic trajectories in reconstructed physics before using them for real-policy training. Their benchmarks are not directly comparable.

    HF-WM combines human interaction and synthetic failure-aware training. Dream to Remember rehearses old skills through a world model, with its use for new-skill learning dependent on fidelity. CASTER formalizes physical/semantic refinement of video-generated trajectories. Together these support hybrid capture, simulation and validation, not the claim that real data is obsolete.
