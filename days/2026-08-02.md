### Data collection

- **OopsieData (announced July 31 by Paul Zhou, amplified by Sergey Levine) is a community effort to collect the robot failure clips that labs currently throw away, framed as "the most abundant but underused resource in robot learning."** Levine's addendum is the demand-side argument: robots make suboptimal data on their own, and the more robots there are, the more of it exists. Every prior signal on this thread (JD Logistics calling failure data severely underpriced, RedFlow converting failures into correction labels) was about private pipelines; this is the first open corpus organized around failure specifically. If it compounds, failure capture, labeling and triage become a spec'd deliverable rather than exhaust. [Levine on X](https://x.com/svlevine)
- **Sharpa Robotics released the Origami Dataset on July 30, a visuotactile corpus of its robots folding origami paper planes, built for the Robotic Origami Challenge at IROS 2026 that Sharpa co-organizes with Chris Paxton, Michael Cho, Jiafei Duan and Lightwheel.** Sharpa calls it one of the largest tactile datasets released. Paxton's framing: touch is the frontier, and folding a paper plane is hard even for humans. The same Sharpa hands sit on NVIDIA's GR00T reference humanoid, so a public tactile benchmark from the hand vendor is also a spec-setting move for what tactile capture should look like. [Paxton on X](https://x.com/chris_j_paxton)

### Data annotation & validation

- **From today, Article 10 of the EU AI Act is enforceable for high-risk AI systems: data sets must be relevant, representative and as error-free as possible, under documented governance covering collection origin and every preparation step, annotation and labeling named explicitly, with compliance demonstrable to authorities on request.** Robotics systems that fall in scope (safety components, machinery, employment screening, critical infrastructure) now carry a legal paper trail requirement on their training data. The direction of travel matters more than today's scope: the first major jurisdiction just converted data provenance and annotation quality from a procurement preference into law, and buyer specs tend to follow regulation across borders. [Article 10](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/10/), [A&O Shearman on high-risk obligations](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-tech/zooming-in-on-ai-10-eu-ai-act-what-are-the-obligations-for-high-risk-ai-systems)
- **Failure Detection for Surgical Robot Imitation Policies via Flow-Matching World Modeling (arXiv 2607.27511, Georgia Tech, submitted July 29) extends the runtime-verification thread into the highest-stakes domain: a flow-matching world model watches a surgical imitation policy and flags failures as they develop.** Two days after CheckVLA published recall and false-alarm numbers for mobile manipulation, the same architecture pattern (separate world model as an always-on checker over the policy) shows up for surgery, where an undetected failure is not a dropped cup. Runtime verification is consolidating into the default safety layer, and every layer of it prices validated ground truth. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27511)

### Robotics & humanoids

- **F.03 climbed a ladder, fully autonomous, per Adcock's August 1 post (347K views inside a day), reposted by Figure's company account.** The capability lineage is the RL locomotion stack Figure has been showing on stairs; a ladder adds hands-and-feet whole-body coordination against a vertical structure. As with every Figure demo, it is a curated company video with no third-party verification, but the cadence (stairs, then hours-long stair endurance, now a ladder) reads like a deliberate legged-mobility argument being assembled in public. [Adcock on X](https://x.com/adcock_brett)
- **Adcock followed the demo with a flat positioning claim: "Wheeled robots are an utter dead end," quote-posting the argument that the world is filled with stairs and ladders.** Two weeks ago Holiday Robotics raised $105M for a wheeled humanoid and Travis Kalanick's ATOMS raised $1.7B explicitly against the humanoid form factor for industrial scale. The form-factor argument is now a funded, three-way public fight: legged generalists, wheeled task robots, and purpose-built non-humanoid automation. [Adcock on X](https://x.com/adcock_brett)
- **PAC-MAN (arXiv 2607.28623, Caltech, Aaron Ames' lab) trains perception-aware whole-body safety for a humanoid via control-barrier-function RL, demonstrated as humanoid dodgeball.** Whole-body evasive behavior under perception constraints is the safety-side complement to the locomotion demos above: knowing what not to get hit by is the same machinery as knowing what not to hit. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28623)

### Research papers worth a look

- **Failure Detection for Surgical Robot Imitation Policies via Flow-Matching World Modeling (arXiv 2607.27511, Georgia Tech).** Full read in annotation above. Runtime verification arrives in surgery. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27511)
- **PAC-MAN: Perception-Aware CBF-RL for Whole-Body Safety in Humanoid Dodgeball (arXiv 2607.28623, Caltech).** Full read in humanoids above. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28623)
- **Static In, Dynamic Out: Counterfactual Action Augmentation for Moving Object Manipulation (arXiv 2607.27890, Georgia Tech, Danfei Xu's group).** Generates counterfactual dynamic-object training data from static demonstrations. Another entry in the ledger of what buyers will synthesize versus what they must capture; motion-rich interaction keeps moving to the synthesized column while contact ground truth stays captured. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27890)
- **FA-RDP: A Frequency-Adaptive Reactive Diffusion Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation (arXiv 2607.28596, Shanghai Jiao Tong, Cewu Lu's group).** Adapts the control frequency of a reactive diffusion policy to the contact phase of a task. The reactive-policy thread from πR² continues: chunked policies are becoming closed-loop, which raises the bar on the timestamp and sync quality of training data. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28596)
- **UniCross: Unified Cross-Skill Dexterous Manipulation Synthesis (arXiv 2607.28198, ETH Zurich).** One framework synthesizing dexterous manipulation across skills, with a project page. Cross-skill synthesis is the dexterity-side version of the demo-count collapse. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28198)
- **FasTac: A Curved Multispectral Vision-Based Tactile Sensor for High-Speed High-Precision 3D Shape and Force Perception (arXiv 2607.28416, Huazhong UST).** New tactile sensor hardware claiming high-speed 3D shape and force. Filed against the standing question of whether touch gets sensed or inferred; the sensor side keeps improving too. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28416)
- **World Action Planner: Generalizable Decision-Making with Action-Conditioned World Models (arXiv 2607.27599, Xiangcheng Zhang and Yilun Du).** Action-conditioned world models as general planners. Same substrate CheckVLA uses for verification, pointed at decision-making. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27599)
- **Sharpa Origami Dataset (released July 30, Sharpa Robotics with Lightwheel, for the IROS 2026 Robotic Origami Challenge).** Full read in collection above. [Paxton on X](https://x.com/chris_j_paxton)
- **OopsieData (announced July 31, community open dataset of robot failure clips).** Full read in collection above. [Levine on X](https://x.com/svlevine)
